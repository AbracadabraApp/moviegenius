# Cinema Genius Test - 30 Multiple Choice Questions

Based on the comprehensive episode content from the Movie Genius database, this test covers key concepts, films, directors, and movements from cinema history.

## Test Questions

**Question 1:** Which film marked the definitive arrival of German Expressionism and featured painted shadows and geometric set designs?
A) Nosferatu (1922)
B) The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari (1920)
C) Metropolis (1927)
D) The Last Laugh (1924)
**Answer: B**

**Question 2:** What innovative camera technique did Hitchcock pioneer in "Vertigo" (1958) to externalize the protagonist's acrophobia?
A) Crane shot
B) Steadicam
C) Dolly zoom (simultaneously dollying backward while zooming in)
D) Handheld camera
**Answer: C**

**Question 3:** Which film established the template for the noir femme fatale with Mary Astor's portrayal of Brigid O'Shaughnessy?
A) Double Indemnity (1944)
B) The Big Sleep (1946)
C) The Maltese Falcon (1941)
D) Gilda (1946)
**Answer: C**

**Question 4:** Jean-Luc Godard's "Breathless" (1960) revolutionized cinema by deliberately violating which established convention?
A) Color cinematography
B) Synchronized sound
C) Narrative structure
D) Editing with jump cuts
**Answer: D**

**Question 5:** What was the name of the progressive rock band that collaborated with Dario Argento on iconic giallo soundtracks?
A) Pink Floyd
B) Goblin
C) King Crimson
D) Yes
**Answer: B**

**Question 6:** Akira Kurosawa's "Seven Samurai" (1954) influenced which major Hollywood film genre?
A) Film noir
B) Westerns
C) Horror films
D) Romantic comedies
**Answer: B**

**Question 7:** Which silent comedy star was known as "The Great Stone Face" for his deadpan expression?
A) Charlie Chaplin
B) Harold Lloyd
C) Buster Keaton
D) Fatty Arbuckle
**Answer: C**

**Question 8:** Fritz Lang's "M" (1931) was groundbreaking for its innovative use of what cinematic element?
A) Color cinematography
B) Sound design and parallel editing
C) 3D effects
D) Slow motion
**Answer: B**

**Question 9:** What film technique did Lois Weber pioneer in "Suspense" (1913), decades before it became common practice?
A) Fade-in/fade-out
B) Close-up shots
C) Three-way split screen
D) Tracking shots
**Answer: C**

**Question 10:** The infamous shower scene in Hitchcock's "Psycho" (1960) contained how many camera setups?
A) 45
B) 62
C) 78
D) 91
**Answer: C**

**Question 11:** Which documentary filmmaker is famous for his "fly-on-the-wall" observational style with no narration or interviews?
A) D.A. Pennebaker
B) Frederick Wiseman
C) Errol Morris
D) Werner Herzog
**Answer: B**

**Question 12:** What cinematographic innovation did Karl Freund develop in "The Last Laugh" (1924)?
A) Deep focus photography
B) Color timing
C) The "unchained camera" (fluid tracking shots)
D) Forced perspective
**Answer: C**

**Question 13:** Which giallo film featured the famous three-minute crane shot that scaled an apartment building's exterior?
A) Profondo Rosso (1975)
B) Suspiria (1977)
C) Tenebrae (1982)
D) Opera (1987)
**Answer: C**

**Question 14:** The French New Wave movement emerged from critics writing for which influential film magazine?
A) Sight & Sound
B) Cahiers du Cinéma
C) Film Quarterly
D) Positif
**Answer: B**

**Question 15:** What special effects technique did Eugen Schüfftan develop for "Metropolis" (1927)?
A) Stop-motion animation
B) Rear projection
C) Using mirrors to combine miniature sets with live actors
D) Optical printing
**Answer: C**

**Question 16:** Which film marked the first collaboration between Akira Kurosawa and Toshiro Mifune?
A) Rashomon (1950)
B) Drunken Angel (1948)
C) Seven Samurai (1954)
D) Yojimbo (1961)
**Answer: B**

**Question 17:** Mary Pickford co-founded which major film production company in 1919?
A) Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer
B) Paramount Pictures
C) United Artists
D) Warner Bros.
**Answer: C**

**Question 18:** Which 1970s film demonstrated that investigative journalism could be turned into gripping entertainment?
A) The Conversation (1974)
B) All the President's Men (1976)
C) The Parallax View (1974)
D) Three Days of the Condor (1975)
**Answer: B**

**Question 19:** What technique did Harold Lloyd use in "Safety Last!" (1923) to create the famous clock-hanging sequence?
A) Rear projection
B) Miniature effects
C) Forced perspective and carefully constructed illusions
D) Stop-motion animation
**Answer: C**

**Question 20:** Which film is considered the first true giallo, establishing the genre's conventions?
A) The Girl Who Knew Too Much (1963)
B) Blood and Black Lace (1964)
C) The Bird with the Crystal Plumage (1970)
D) Profondo Rosso (1975)
**Answer: A**

**Question 21:** The "Vertigo effect" technique has been referenced in which of the following films?
A) Jaws (1975)
B) Goodfellas (1990)
C) Both A and B
D) Neither A nor B
**Answer: C**

**Question 22:** What was controversial about Frederick Wiseman's debut documentary "Titicut Follies" (1967)?
A) It was filmed without permission
B) It was banned in Massachusetts until 1991
C) It used hidden cameras
D) It featured staged scenes
**Answer: B**

**Question 23:** Which cinematographer won an Oscar for his work on "The Third Man" (1949)?
A) Gregg Toland
B) Robert Krasker
C) James Wong Howe
D) Leon Shamroy
**Answer: B**

**Question 24:** François Truffaut's "The 400 Blows" (1959) was notable for its use of what filming technique?
A) Handheld cameras and natural lighting
B) Studio sets and artificial lighting
C) Animation sequences
D) Multiple exposure effects
**Answer: A**

**Question 25:** What innovation did Mario Bava introduce in "Blood and Black Lace" (1964) that became a giallo staple?
A) Extreme close-ups
B) The murderer's black-gloved hands
C) Slow-motion sequences
D) Underwater photography
**Answer: B**

**Question 26:** Which film directly inspired George Lucas's "Star Wars" in terms of narrative structure?
A) Seven Samurai (1954)
B) The Hidden Fortress (1958)
C) Yojimbo (1961)
D) Rashomon (1950)
**Answer: B**

**Question 27:** What was the maximum budget for Kurosawa's "Ran" (1985), enormous for Japanese cinema at the time?
A) $6 million
B) $8 million
C) $12 million
D) $18 million
**Answer: C**

**Question 28:** Which film featured Charlie Chaplin's famous "bread roll dance" sequence?
A) The Kid (1921)
B) The Gold Rush (1925)
C) City Lights (1931)
D) Modern Times (1936)
**Answer: B**

**Question 29:** The Italian neorealist movement was influenced by which German Expressionist director's techniques?
A) Fritz Lang
B) F.W. Murnau
C) Lois Weber
D) Robert Wiene
**Answer: C**

**Question 30:** What film launched the Spaghetti Western genre as a remake of a Kurosawa film?
A) The Good, the Bad and the Ugly
B) A Fistful of Dollars (remake of Yojimbo)
C) For a Few Dollars More
D) Once Upon a Time in the West
**Answer: B**
