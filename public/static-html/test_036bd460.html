Set in Soviet Russia, 'We the Living' follows Kira, a young woman whose bourgeois family returns to Petrograd after the revolution. The film daringly portrays her struggle between love for Leo, an aristocrat's son, and Andre, a Communist officer who can protect her. Through this triangle, the story explores individual desires against collective demands, making it both a passionate romance and political commentary. The narrative's complexity lies in how it mirrors the novel's anti-collectivist themes while navigating fascist Italy's censorship.
Alida Valli delivers a breakthrough performance as Kira, channeling both vulnerability and fierce independence reminiscent of Ingrid Bergman in Casablanca (1942). Director Alessandrini, working within constraints, creates a visual language that would later influence Italian Neorealism, particularly visible in films like Rome, Open City (1945). The film's style bridges classical melodrama with emerging realist techniques, showing remarkable innovation for its era.
Originally released as two films in Italy, 'Noi Vivi' and 'Addio Kira,' the work was banned once authorities realized its anti-authoritarian message could apply to any totalitarian regime. Its themes of individual rights versus state control echo through later films like Doctor Zhivago (1965) and The Lives of Others (2006). The film's restoration in the 1980s revealed its significance as both historical document and artistic achievement.
Within 1940s political cinema, 'We the Living' stands unique for smuggling anti-totalitarian themes past censors, similar to how To Be or Not to Be (1942) used comedy to critique Nazism. Its influence can be traced through later European resistance films like Army of Shadows (1969) and modern political dramas such as The Death of Stalin (2017).
The film's exploration of personal freedom versus political control remains startlingly relevant. Its themes resonate with contemporary works like V for Vendetta (2005) and Snowpiercer (2013), while its depiction of love under political pressure parallels modern films like Cold War (2018). The restoration and rediscovery of 'We the Living' offers vital lessons about artistic resistance and political filmmaking.
