Set in 1971 Mumbai during the Indo-Pakistani War, 'Such a Long Journey' follows Gustad Noble, a bank clerk whose ordinary life becomes entangled in a web of political intrigue. When his old friend Jimmy asks him to help with a covert operation involving Prime Minister Indira Gandhi's government, Gustad finds himself caught between duty, friendship, and moral conscience. The story weaves together personal struggles with national crisis, as Gustad also deals with his son's rebellion, his daughter's illness, and the transformation of his beloved neighborhood.
Roshan Seth's nuanced portrayal of Gustad anchors the film with quiet dignity reminiscent of Ben Kingsley in Gandhi (1982). Om Puri brings gravitas to his supporting role, continuing his pattern of powerful character work seen in City of Joy (1992). Director Sturla Gunnarsson crafts an intimate narrative that recalls the careful pacing of Satyajit Ray's The World of Apu (1959), while maintaining a contemporary edge in its political commentary.
The film masterfully depicts the Parsi community in Mumbai, offering rare insight into this distinct cultural group while exploring universal themes of loyalty and change. Like Monsoon Wedding (2001), it balances family dynamics with broader social commentary. The political backdrop of Emergency-era India creates tension similar to that found in The Year of Living Dangerously (1982), where personal lives intersect with historical moments.
As a literary adaptation, the film joins works like The Namesake (2006) in examining the Indian diaspora and cultural identity. Its handling of political intrigue through the lens of ordinary lives recalls The Lunchbox (2013), though with more overt historical context. The film's attention to community life and social change echoes themes found in Earth (1998).
The film's exploration of government overreach and individual moral choices remains startlingly relevant. Its themes of maintaining personal integrity in politically charged times resonate with contemporary global situations, while its portrayal of community bonds in urban life speaks to ongoing issues of development and preservation in modern cities.
