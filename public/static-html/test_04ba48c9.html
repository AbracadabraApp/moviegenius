Set in New York's Grand Central Terminal, this adaptation of Mary Higgins Clark's novel follows a psychopath who kidnaps both a young girl and her father's girlfriend. The story attempts to build tension through its confined setting and psychological warfare, focusing on the relationship between the victims and their captor. While the premise promises intense suspense, the execution often misses opportunities to create genuine thrills.
Rip Torn's performance as the antagonist occasionally shows glimpses of menace but never fully commits to the darkness the role demands. Kate Mulgrew, in an early role before her Star Trek: Voyager fame, brings some credibility to her character, though the script gives her limited room to develop. Director Sean S. Cunningham, fresh off his success with Friday the 13th (1980), struggles to translate his slasher expertise into psychological thriller territory.
The film emerged during a period when Hollywood was heavily invested in urban crime thrillers, following successes like Death Wish (1974) and The Taking of Pelham One Two Three (1974). It attempts to tap into growing anxieties about urban safety and stranger danger in early 1980s New York, though without the sociological depth of its contemporaries.
Within the thriller genre, 'A Stranger is Watching' sits awkwardly between the gritty urban crime films of the 1970s and the more polished psychological thrillers that would emerge later in the 1980s with films like Fatal Attraction (1987). It lacks both the raw energy of the former and the sophisticated plotting of the latter.
While the film's themes of urban violence and kidnapping remain relevant, its dated execution and pacing issues make it primarily of interest to genre historians and completists studying the evolution of the American thriller. Modern viewers might find more satisfaction in contemporary takes on similar themes like Panic Room (2002) or Gone Girl (2014).
