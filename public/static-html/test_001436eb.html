Jack Lemmon stars as Stanley Ford, a successful newspaper cartoonist living the bachelor dream life in Manhattan with his loyal butler Charles (Terry-Thomas). His perfectly ordered world turns upside down when he drunkenly marries a beautiful Italian woman (Virna Lisi) who pops out of a cake at a bachelor party. Unable to get an annulment and unwilling to adapt to married life, Stanley begins plotting his wife's murder through his newspaper comic strip, leading to increasingly complicated and hilarious situations as fiction and reality begin to blur.
Lemmon delivers one of his most engaging performances, channeling the same everyman frustration that made The Apartment (1960) so memorable. His chemistry with Virna Lisi creates genuine romantic tension beneath the comedy. Director Richard Quine maintains a breezy tone while building the farcical elements to a clever climax, much like his work in Bell, Book and Candle (1958).
The film serves as a fascinating time capsule of 1960s gender politics, playing with themes that would soon be challenged by second-wave feminism. While some attitudes feel dated, the core commentary about marriage, independence, and compromise remains surprisingly sharp. The film shares DNA with other marriage-themed comedies of the era like Marriage on the Rocks (1965) and Divorce American Style (1967).
Coming at the tail end of the romantic comedy golden age, the film bridges the sophisticated comedy of Pillow Talk (1959) with the more satirical edge emerging in films like The Graduate (1967). Its blend of slapstick and social commentary influenced later marriage-centered comedies from The War of the Roses (1989) to Crazy, Stupid, Love (2011).
While some elements feel products of their time, the film's core observations about relationship compromise and the challenges of maintaining identity within marriage remain relevant. The sharp writing and Lemmon's performance help elevate it beyond simple period piece status to something that still resonates with modern viewers grappling with similar relationship dynamics.
