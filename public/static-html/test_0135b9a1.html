Set across 24 hectic hours in a New York City newsroom, 'The Paper' follows metro editor Henry Hackett (Michael Keaton) as he juggles a career-changing job offer, his pregnant wife (Marisa Tomei), and a breaking story about two wrongly accused teenagers. The film captures the last glory days of print journalism, when deadlines were absolute and the morning edition could make or break careers. Keaton's Hackett embodies the passionate journalist who believes truth matters more than convenience, clashing with managing editor Alicia Clark (Glenn Close) who prioritizes business over accuracy.
Ron Howard directs with the same attention to workplace dynamics that made Apollo 13 (1995) so compelling. The ensemble cast delivers perfectly calibrated performances, with Keaton's manic energy balanced by Glenn Close's ice-queen efficiency and Robert Duvall's old-school wisdom as the veteran editor-in-chief. The film's kinetic camera work and overlapping dialogue create a sense of constant motion reminiscent of classic newsroom films like His Girl Friday (1940).
Released just before the internet would transform journalism forever, 'The Paper' serves as both a celebration and elegy for print journalism. Like Broadcast News (1987) before it and Spotlight (2015) after, it explores the tension between journalistic integrity and commercial pressures. The film's themes of racial prejudice in news coverage and the rush to judgment remain startlingly relevant in today's 24/7 news cycle.
The film follows in the tradition of great journalism movies like All the President's Men (1976) but adds a distinct workplace comedy element that would later influence films like Morning Glory (2010). It successfully combines the rapid-fire dialogue of classic newspaper films with the emerging 1990s workplace ensemble drama format seen in films like Working Girl (1988).
In an era of 'fake news' accusations and declining print media, 'The Paper' reminds us what good journalism requires: dedication, integrity, and the courage to admit mistakes. Its themes of work-life balance and ethical decision-making under pressure resonate even more strongly today, making it a fascinating bridge between old-school journalism and contemporary media challenges.
