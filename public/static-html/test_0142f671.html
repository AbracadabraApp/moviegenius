Sisters follows Kate (Tina Fey) and Maura Ellis (Amy Poehler), two siblings who discover their parents are selling their childhood home. In response, they decide to throw one last epic house party, reversing their teenage roles - with party-girl Kate playing responsible supervisor while straight-laced Maura finally lets loose. The premise might sound simple, but it's elevated by the genuine sisterly chemistry between Fey and Poehler, who bring both hilarity and heart to their roles. Their relationship feels authentic, messy, and ultimately touching.
The film thrives on Fey and Poehler's established rapport from their SNL days and previous collaborations like Baby Mama (2008). Director Jason Moore, fresh from Pitch Perfect (2012), knows how to orchestrate ensemble comedy chaos while maintaining emotional stakes. The supporting cast, including fellow SNL alums Maya Rudolph and Bobby Moynihan, delivers memorable character moments that enhance the main duo's performances.
Sisters cleverly subverts the typical male-centric party movie format seen in films like Project X (2012) and Old School (2003), putting women in their 40s at the center of the chaos. It tackles themes of arrested development and mid-life crisis with surprising depth, while never losing its comedic edge. The film joins movies like Bridesmaids (2011) in proving that female-led comedies can be both commercially successful and genuinely funny.
While it follows in the footsteps of classic party films like Animal House (1978) and House Party (1990), Sisters modernizes the formula by focusing on middle-aged characters facing real-life transitions. It shares DNA with other contemporary comedies about delayed adulthood like Step Brothers (2008) while maintaining its own unique voice.
The film's themes of accepting change, confronting aging, and maintaining sibling relationships remain relevant. Its success paved the way for more female-driven comedies and helped establish a new sub-genre of adult party movies, influencing films like Girls Trip (2017) and Book Club (2018).
