This ingenious satire follows NRI businessman Om Shastri, who returns to India after losing everything in the 2008 American financial crisis, only to be kidnapped by a gang of small-time criminals. The twist? These kidnappers are also suffering from the global recession as wealthy targets become scarce. The film cleverly weaves multiple narrative threads as different gangs try to profit from Om's kidnapping, not realizing he's actually broke. The story becomes a masterful commentary on how global economic forces affect even local criminal enterprises.
Rajat Kapoor delivers a nuanced performance as Om, perfectly capturing the desperation of a failed businessman while maintaining subtle comedic timing. Director Subhash Kapoor crafts a narrative that feels both authentically Indian and globally relevant, similar to how 3 Idiots (2009) balanced local flavor with universal themes. The supporting cast, particularly Manu Rishi as the English-speaking gangster, brings depth to what could have been caricatured roles.
The film brilliantly captures the ripple effects of the 2008 global financial crisis on unexpected corners of society. Like The Big Short (2015) did for Wall Street, this film uses humor to explain complex economic concepts. It's particularly effective in showing how globalization connects seemingly unrelated worlds - American banking and small-town Indian kidnapping gangs. The film also cleverly satirizes India's obsession with America and the English language.
Within Indian cinema, this film marked a significant shift toward intelligent satire, following the path paved by films like Khosla Ka Ghosla (2006) and Oye Lucky! Lucky Oye! (2008). It demonstrated that comedy could be both meaningful and entertaining without relying on slapstick humor. The film's influence can be seen in later works like Newton (2017) and Article 15 (2019), which also blend social commentary with engaging narratives.
The film's themes of economic uncertainty and globalization's impact on local communities remain startlingly relevant, especially in post-pandemic times. Its commentary on the interconnectedness of global economies and local livelihoods echoes in contemporary discussions about economic inequality and crisis management.
