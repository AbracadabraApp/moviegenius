Following the devastating events of Man of Steel (2013), Batman v Superman positions two iconic heroes as ideological opponents. Bruce Wayne views Superman as an unchecked alien threat, while Clark Kent questions Batman's brutal vigilante justice. Their conflict emerges from genuine philosophical differences about power and responsibility, but gets lost in a maze of subplots involving Lex Luthor's manipulations, apocalyptic dreams, and rushed Justice League setups. The introduction of Wonder Woman provides a bright spot, though her presence feels more calculated for future franchising than organic to this story.
Ben Affleck delivers a compelling portrayal of an older, more cynical Batman, channeling the darker tone of The Dark Knight Returns (1986) comic series. Henry Cavill's Superman remains stoic but struggles with limited emotional range. Gal Gadot's Wonder Woman steals her scenes, while Jesse Eisenberg's twitchy, manic Lex Luthor divides audiences. Snyder's signature visual style - reminiscent of 300 (2006) - creates striking imagery but often prioritizes slow-motion spectacle over storytelling clarity.
The film attempts to grapple with post-9/11 themes of security versus freedom, echoing debates from The Dark Knight (2008). Its exploration of media influence and public perception of heroes parallels real-world discussions about power and accountability. However, these weightier themes get buried under excessive CGI destruction and muddled plotting that recalls Transformers (2007) more than thoughtful superhero commentary.
Released amid the superhero genre's peak, BvS aimed to differentiate itself with a darker, more serious tone. While The Avengers (2012) embraced comic book joy, Snyder's film followed Watchmen (2009) in deconstructing hero worship. This grimmer approach influenced later films like Logan (2017), though most subsequent DC films would pivot toward lighter tones following audience response.
The film's themes of unchecked power and hero accountability remain relevant, though better explored in works like The Boys (2019-). Its influence on superhero cinema lies more in what to avoid - overcrowded plotting, excessive darkness, and rushed universe building - than positive innovations. Its reception helped reshape DC's subsequent film strategy.
