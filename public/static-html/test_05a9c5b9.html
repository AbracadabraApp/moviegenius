In a Budapest slaughterhouse, two seemingly incompatible souls discover they share the same dreams at night, where they meet as deer in a snow-covered forest. Mária, a quality inspector with apparent autism-spectrum traits, and Endre, the financial director with a physically disabled arm, navigate their unusual connection in both the dream world and harsh reality. Their story unfolds with delicate precision, mixing the mundane with the magical in ways that echo the surreal tenderness of Amelie (2001) while maintaining its own distinct voice.
Alexandra Borbély delivers a breakthrough performance as Mária, bringing remarkable nuance to a character who struggles with human connection. Her portrayal recalls the careful precision of Rooney Mara in The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo (2011), but with a gentler, more vulnerable edge. Director Ildikó Enyedi returns to filmmaking after an 18-year hiatus, bringing the same magical sensibility that marked her earlier work My Twentieth Century (1989), while crafting something entirely contemporary.
The film masterfully addresses modern alienation and the search for connection in an increasingly mechanized world. Like Her (2013), it explores unconventional romance in the modern age, but through a distinctly European art-house lens. The slaughterhouse setting provides a stark backdrop that contrasts beautifully with the ethereal dream sequences, creating a meditation on the intersection of the physical and spiritual that recalls The Shape of Water (2017).
Within the tradition of magical realist romance, On Body and Soul stands alongside films like Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind (2004) and Wings of Desire (1987), but carves its own unique path. The film's approach to dreams and reality bears similarity to Uncle Boonmee Who Can Recall His Past Lives (2010), though with a more grounded European sensibility.
In an era of digital relationships and social isolation, the film's exploration of genuine human connection resonates deeply. Its treatment of neurodiversity and physical disability through a romantic lens offers a refreshing perspective that challenges conventional romance narratives, similar to how The Theory of Everything (2014) approached love through the lens of disability, but with a more experimental artistic vision.
