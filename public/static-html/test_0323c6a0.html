In a small Southern town, pregnant pie-maker Jenna crafts delectable desserts while trapped in an unhappy marriage with her controlling husband Earl. Her life takes an unexpected turn when she begins an affair with her new gynecologist Dr. Pomatter, while her creative pie-making becomes both her escape and her means of self-expression. Each pie she creates - with names like 'I Hate My Husband Pie' and 'Baby Screaming Its Head Off in the Middle of the Night and Ruining My Life Pie' - serves as a window into her inner world.
Keri Russell shines in a role that requires both vulnerability and quiet strength, reminiscent of Sally Field's performance in Norma Rae (1979). The late Adrienne Shelly's direction brings a gentle touch to difficult subjects, creating a world that feels both realistic and slightly magical, similar to the tone achieved in Chocolat (2000). Andy Griffith's final film performance as Old Joe provides a perfect blend of crusty wisdom and unexpected tenderness.
The film tackles serious themes of domestic abuse, female friendship, and economic independence while maintaining its warmth and humor. Like Alice Doesn't Live Here Anymore (1974), it presents a working-class woman's struggle for autonomy without descending into melodrama. The diner setting becomes a microcosm of female solidarity, echoing similar themes found in Fried Green Tomatoes (1991).
Waitress stands out in the indie comedy-drama genre by mixing elements of culinary films like Julie & Julia (2009) with the emotional depth of character studies like Girl, Interrupted (1999). The film's approach to food as emotional expression predates similar themes in Chef (2014), while its exploration of workplace camaraderie recalls 9 to 5 (1980).
The film's messages about female empowerment and economic independence remain deeply relevant, while its exploration of toxic relationships and the courage to leave them resonates with contemporary discussions about domestic abuse. Its success led to a hit Broadway musical adaptation, proving the enduring appeal of its core themes.
