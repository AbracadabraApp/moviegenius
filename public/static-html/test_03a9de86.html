Following her sister's tragic death, Nic and her friends attempt to heal through a kayaking trip that turns into a nightmare when a great white shark begins stalking them. While the premise attempts to blend personal trauma with survival horror, the execution falls short of creating genuine emotional resonance. The characters, though competently performed, never develop beyond standard horror film archetypes, making it difficult to invest in their fate.
Teressa Liane delivers a serviceable performance as Nic, but the script gives her limited material to work with. Director Andrew Traucki, who also helmed the original The Reef (2010), attempts to recreate the tension of his earlier work but struggles to find fresh angles in the shark attack subgenre. The underwater cinematography, while technically proficient, lacks the innovative approaches seen in films like 47 Meters Down (2017).
The film attempts to explore themes of trauma and sisterhood, but these elements feel superficial compared to more successful horror-drama hybrids like The Babadook (2014). While there's an admirable attempt to connect environmental conservation with horror elements, the message gets lost in conventional genre trappings.
In the post-Jaws (1975) landscape of shark films, The Reef: Stalked adds little to the conversation. Unlike recent entries like The Shallows (2016) or Deep Blue Sea (1999), which found creative ways to reinvent the formula, this sequel plays it safe with familiar beats and predictable scares.
Despite growing environmental concerns about shark conservation and increased interest in survival horror, the film misses opportunities to meaningfully engage with either topic. Where Open Water (2003) created lasting impact through minimalist realism, The Reef: Stalked relies too heavily on conventional thriller elements without adding substantive commentary.
