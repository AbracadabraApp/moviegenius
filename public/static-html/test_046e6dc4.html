That's My Boy follows Donny Berger (Adam Sandler), who became a teen father after an inappropriate relationship with his teacher. Years later, the now-broke Donny attempts to reconnect with his estranged son Todd (Andy Samberg) before his wedding for financial gain. The premise immediately stumbles by treating statutory rape as comedy fodder, setting a problematic tone that the film never overcomes. The story attempts to build toward father-son reconciliation but gets lost in a sea of crass jokes and uncomfortable scenarios.
Sandler adopts an abrasive, over-the-top Boston accent and leans into his familiar man-child persona, but without the charm that made characters in Billy Madison (1995) or Happy Gilmore (1996) endearing. Samberg, fresh from SNL success, feels constrained by a straight-man role that doesn't utilize his unique comedic talents. Their potential chemistry gets buried under layers of crude humor and forced conflict. Director Sean Anders, who would later show more heart in Instant Family (2018), struggles to find the right balance between raunch and sentiment.
Released in 2012, the film represents the declining phase of Sandler's broad comedy era, following the pattern of diminishing returns seen in Jack and Jill (2011). Its approach to sensitive topics and reliance on shock value humor feels particularly tone-deaf, especially compared to more nuanced contemporary comedies like 21 Jump Street (2012) or Bridesmaids (2011). The film's treatment of serious issues as punchlines reflects poorly on early 2010s comedy standards.
Within the R-rated comedy landscape, That's My Boy arrived when the genre was evolving beyond simple shock value toward more character-driven narratives, as seen in The Hangover (2009) and Ted (2012). While these films successfully balanced crude humor with heart, That's My Boy remains stuck in an outdated approach that feels more aligned with the less sophisticated comedies of the previous decade.
The film's attempts at humor through inappropriate relationships and casual misogyny feel even more problematic today. Modern comedies like Game Night (2018) and Palm Springs (2020) demonstrate how to create R-rated entertainment without punching down or relying on dated stereotypes. That's My Boy serves as a cautionary tale about comedy that ages poorly due to its insensitive approach to serious subjects.
