This searing documentary investigates private military contractors like Blackwater, CACI, and Halliburton/KBR during the Iraq War, revealing how these corporations profited enormously from the conflict while often providing substandard services that put both military personnel and civilians at risk. Through intimate interviews with former employees, family members of those killed, and military officials, the film builds a devastating case about the human cost of war privatization.
Director Robert Greenwald employs a direct, no-frills documentary approach that prioritizes testimony and evidence over stylistic flourishes. The film's power comes from its whistleblowers and subjects, who provide firsthand accounts of corporate negligence and corruption. The editing masterfully weaves together complex financial data with emotional personal stories, creating both an intellectual and visceral impact.
Released during the height of the Iraq War, this documentary helped shape public understanding of military privatization and its consequences. Like Fahrenheit 9/11 (2004), it challenged the official narrative of the war, but focused specifically on corporate profiteering rather than political decisions. The film continues to resonate as a warning about the dangers of privatizing military operations and the lack of accountability in modern warfare.
Following in the tradition of investigative documentaries like The Corporation (2003) and Enron: The Smartest Guys in the Room (2005), the film exemplifies how documentary filmmaking can serve as a tool for corporate accountability and public awareness. It shares DNA with later works like Inside Job (2010) in its detailed exposure of systemic corruption and its human consequences.
The issues raised in 'Iraq for Sale' remain pressingly relevant, as private military contractors continue to play major roles in modern conflicts. The film's examination of corporate accountability and military privatization connects to ongoing debates about the role of private companies in national security, similar to themes explored in Vice (2018) and The Report (2019).
