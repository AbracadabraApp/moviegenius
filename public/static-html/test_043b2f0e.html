Set in a dystopian 2095 New York, Immortel follows the Egyptian god Horus who has seven days to find a host body and mate with the mysterious blue-haired woman Jill. The film blends Egyptian mythology with cyberpunk aesthetics, creating a unique but often bewildering narrative. The story attempts to tackle themes of immortality, genetic manipulation, and corporate control, but struggles to make these elements cohere into a satisfying whole.
Director Enki Bilal adapts his own graphic novel series, bringing his distinctive visual style to life through a pioneering but uneven mix of CGI characters and live actors. Linda Hardy's performance as Jill feels constrained by the technical limitations, while Thomas Kretschmann's fully CGI character lacks emotional resonance. The film's ambitious visual effects, groundbreaking for 2004, now appear dated and contribute to the narrative disconnect.
The film reflects early 2000s anxieties about genetic modification, corporate power, and technological advancement. Like Blade Runner (1982) and Ghost in the Shell (1995), it explores transhumanism and identity in a cyberpunk future, but lacks their philosophical depth. The mixture of ancient mythology and futuristic elements echoes themes from Metropolis (1927), though with less coherent social commentary.
Immortel represents an interesting experiment in French science fiction cinema, following the tradition of La Jetée (1962) and preceding Renaissance (2006). Its ambitious mixing of CGI and live action prefigured later experiments like Sky Captain and the World of Tomorrow (2004), though its execution remains problematic. The film's visual style influenced later European sci-fi but its narrative shortcomings limited its impact.
While its themes of corporate control and genetic manipulation remain relevant, the film's dated effects and convoluted storytelling diminish its modern appeal. Contemporary works like Ex Machina (2014) and Blade Runner 2049 (2017) handle similar themes with greater clarity and emotional resonance.
