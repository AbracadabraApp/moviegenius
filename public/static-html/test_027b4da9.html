The Reef follows Pi, a young fish who leaves Boston's waters for exotic reef life in the Caribbean. After falling for the beautiful Cordelia, Pi must face off against Troy, a hostile shark who terrorizes the reef's inhabitants. While the story echoes familiar beats from Finding Nemo (2003) and Shark Tale (2004), it lacks their originality and emotional depth.
The voice performances, while earnest, struggle with an uneven script. Freddie Prinze Jr. brings youthful energy to Pi, and Evan Rachel Wood adds charm as Cordelia, but Rob Schneider's comic relief feels forced. The animation quality falls noticeably short of contemporary standards set by Cars (2006) and Happy Feet (2006) released the same year.
The film attempts to address themes of environmental conservation and marine ecosystem protection, but does so superficially compared to more nuanced treatments in films like Deep Blue (2003) and The Blue Planet (2001). Its messages about bullying and standing up for oneself feel formulaic rather than insightful.
Released during the boom of computer-animated features, The Reef arrived when audiences had already experienced masterful underwater animation in Finding Nemo (2003). It fails to innovate or distinguish itself in a crowded market that included sophisticated offerings like Over the Hedge (2006) and Ice Age: The Meltdown (2006).
While marine conservation remains crucial today, modern audiences have access to superior animated films addressing environmental themes, such as Wall-E (2008) and Moana (2016). The Reef's dated animation and storytelling make it difficult to recommend when better options exist.
