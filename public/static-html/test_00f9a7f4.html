Twin sisters Delphine and Solange dream of finding love and leaving their provincial coastal town of Rochefort. Delphine teaches dance while Solange composes music, both yearning for artistic fulfillment in Paris. Through a series of near-misses and coincidences worthy of classic farce, they cross paths with an American pianist (Gene Kelly), a passionate musician (Michel Piccoli), and a gallery owner who might be Delphine's ideal match. The story unfolds over one magical weekend when a traveling carnival arrives, bringing with it the possibility of romance and escape.
Catherine Deneuve and her real-life sister Françoise Dorléac radiate charm and sophistication, their natural chemistry lending authenticity to their roles. Gene Kelly's presence bridges the gap between French and American musical traditions, while Michel Legrand's jazz-inflected score creates a uniquely sophisticated sound that differs from both Broadway and traditional French chanson. Demy's direction is a masterclass in using color and movement, with every frame composed like a painting in motion.
Released at the height of the French New Wave, the film represents a joyous rebellion against both Hollywood conventions and the often-serious tone of contemporary French cinema. It celebrates provincial French life while simultaneously dreaming of broader horizons, capturing the optimistic spirit of the 1960s. The film's treatment of romance and artistic ambition speaks to universal desires for self-expression and connection.
While Singin' in the Rain (1952) defined the American musical's golden age, and West Side Story (1961) brought grittier realism to the genre, 'Young Girls' creates something entirely new. It builds on the foundation of An American in Paris (1951) while incorporating the freedom and style of the French New Wave, paving the way for modern musical hybrid films like La La Land (2016).
The film's exploration of artistic dreams, romantic ideals, and the tension between provincial life and cosmopolitan ambitions remains startlingly relevant. Its influence can be seen in contemporary works like Moulin Rouge! (2001) and The Umbrellas of Cherbourg (1964), showing how its innovative blend of styles continues to inspire filmmakers.
