Set against the backdrop of pre-WWII London, Mrs. Henderson Presents follows wealthy widow Laura Henderson who purchases the Windmill Theatre on a whim. Partnering with theater manager Vivian Van Damm (Bob Hoskins), she transforms the struggling venue into London's most famous nude revue show. The film chronicles their tumultuous professional relationship and the theater's determination to remain open during the Blitz, famously never closing despite the bombing of London.
Judi Dench embodies Mrs. Henderson with the perfect blend of aristocratic hauteur and mischievous rebellion, earning an Oscar nomination for her performance. Her chemistry with Bob Hoskins creates a compelling dynamic that drives the narrative. Stephen Frears directs with his characteristic attention to period detail and human complexity, crafting scenes that move seamlessly between comedy and wartime gravity.
The film explores themes of female empowerment and artistic expression during wartime, showing how entertainment served as crucial morale support during WWII. It tackles the peculiarly British approach to sexuality and censorship, while also highlighting the role of theater in maintaining civilian spirits during the Blitz. The story provides insight into a unique moment in entertainment history when prudish British society made exceptions to nudity laws for the sake of art and morale.
As a period comedy-drama, the film follows in the tradition of Calendar Girls (2003) and The Full Monty (1997), combining British humor with social commentary. Like Their Finest (2016), it examines the intersection of entertainment and wartime resilience, while sharing DNA with Shakespeare in Love (1998) in its backstage drama and period setting.
The themes of art's importance during crisis and female entrepreneurship remain relevant today. Mrs. Henderson's story of breaking social conventions and fighting bureaucracy resonates with modern audiences, while the film's exploration of entertainment as resistance speaks to contemporary discussions about art's role in difficult times.
