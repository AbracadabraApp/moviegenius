This Indian documentary explores the lives of visually impaired students in Delhi, examining their daily challenges and aspirations. The film follows several students through their academic year, showcasing their determination to overcome societal barriers and achieve their educational goals despite their disabilities.
As a documentary, the film relies on its authentic subjects rather than performances. The cinematography adopts an observational style, though technical limitations of the era and budget constraints are evident. The direction focuses on intimate moments that reveal the students' personalities and struggles.
The film addresses important issues regarding disability rights and education access in India during the early 2000s. While its intentions are admirable, the documentary's impact was limited by its modest distribution and somewhat conventional approach to the subject matter.
Coming in the wake of more prominent social issue documentaries like Born Into Brothels (2004) and The Story of the Weeping Camel (2003), this film attempts to bring attention to disability rights but lacks the technical polish and narrative drive of its contemporaries.
While the issues of disability rights and educational access remain relevant, more recent documentaries like Crip Camp (2020) and Rising Phoenix (2020) offer more comprehensive and contemporary examinations of disability rights and inclusion.
