Set in a dystopian 2050 America, this remake follows professional racer Frankenstein in a cross-country death race where contestants earn points by killing pedestrians. The film attempts to modernize the 1975 original's satirical premise by incorporating virtual reality elements and commentary on digital culture. Manu Bennett's Frankenstein lacks the menacing presence of David Carradine's original portrayal, while Malcolm McDowell's Chairman delivers an over-the-top performance that misses the mark on genuine satire.
The film struggles to balance its B-movie roots with modern special effects and social commentary. While the original Death Race 2000 (1975) succeeded through practical effects and genuine punk attitude, this version relies too heavily on cheap CGI and forced humor. The performances range from earnest attempts by Bennett to cartoonish overacting, never finding the sweet spot between camp and commentary that made the original work.
Where the original film cleverly satirized violence in media and American bloodsport culture, Death Race 2050 attempts to tackle modern issues like reality TV obsession, corporate control, and virtual reality addiction. However, its commentary feels heavy-handed compared to similar modern satirical works like Black Mirror (2011) or The Hunger Games (2012). The film's attempts to critique digital-age violence and entertainment fall flat without the subtle edge of its predecessor.
Within the dystopian action genre, Death Race 2050 fails to innovate or meaningfully build upon established tropes. Films like Mad Max: Fury Road (2015) and Snowpiercer (2013) have shown how to successfully modernize retro-future concepts with both style and substance. Even B-movie homages like Turbo Kid (2015) better capture the spirit of 1970s exploitation cinema while adding contemporary flair.
Despite addressing relevant themes about media consumption and virtual reality, the film's execution undermines its potential impact. Modern classics like Ready Player One (2018) and Gamer (2009) handle similar subject matter with more finesse. The original Death Race's influence can be seen in everything from The Running Man (1987) to Battle Royale (2000), but this remake adds little to the conversation.
