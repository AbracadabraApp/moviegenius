Lupin III: The First marks a groundbreaking moment in the legendary franchise's history, transitioning the beloved gentleman thief into stunning 3D animation while maintaining his characteristic charm and wit. The story follows Lupin's pursuit of the mysterious Bresson Diary, a treasure that eluded even his grandfather. Alongside his familiar companions Jigen and Goemon, and with the addition of a young archaeology student named Laetitia, Lupin races against a sinister organization with ties to the Nazi regime. The plot masterfully weaves together classic Lupin heist elements with larger-scale adventure storytelling reminiscent of Raiders of the Lost Ark (1981).
Director Takashi Yamazaki demonstrates remarkable skill in translating Monkey Punch's iconic character designs into fluid, expressive 3D models. The animation quality rivals works from studios like Pixar, while maintaining the distinctive style that made The Castle of Cagliostro (1979) a classic. The voice performances, particularly Kanichi Kurita as Lupin, bring the perfect mix of swagger and humor that fans expect. The action sequences are choreographed with the precision of Mission: Impossible - Fallout (2018) while keeping the playful energy of classic anime.
The film represents a significant evolution in Japanese animation's global reach, following the path blazed by films like Your Name (2016) and Spirited Away (2001). It successfully modernizes a character that has been entertaining audiences since the 1960s, proving that classic characters can evolve without losing their essential appeal. The story's themes of legacy and responsibility add depth to what could have been a simple heist narrative.
Within the adventure-heist genre, The First stands alongside animated capers like The Great Mouse Detective (1986) and Castle in the Sky (1986), combining intricate plotting with family-friendly excitement. The film demonstrates how 3D animation can honor traditional 2D aesthetics while creating new possibilities for dynamic action and expression, similar to how Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse (2018) revolutionized superhero animation.
In an era where streaming platforms have made anime more accessible than ever, Lupin III: The First serves as both a celebration of the medium's history and a showcase of its future potential. The film's blend of classic character work with cutting-edge animation techniques provides a blueprint for how other classic properties might evolve for modern audiences.
