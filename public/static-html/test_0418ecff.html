Don Ready (Jeremy Piven) leads a team of mercenary car salespeople who travel to struggling dealerships to boost sales. When they arrive at a failing dealership in Temecula, they encounter the usual mix of oddball characters and outrageous situations. The plot follows familiar beats of the underdog story mixed with raunchy comedy, but never quite finds its own identity among similar films of the era.
Jeremy Piven brings his signature fast-talking energy from Entourage (2004-2011) to the lead role, but the material doesn't match his capabilities. The supporting cast, including Ving Rhames and Ed Helms, deliver committed performances that occasionally rise above the script's limitations. First-time director Neal Brennan, co-creator of Chappelle's Show (2003-2006), struggles to find the right tone between outrageous comedy and character development.
Released during the 2009 financial crisis, the film attempts to tap into anxieties about American capitalism and car culture, but lacks the sharp satire of films like Used Cars (1980) or the emotional depth of Tommy Boy (1995). Its commentary on sales culture feels shallow compared to the biting critique of Glengarry Glen Ross (1992).
The film arrived at the tail end of the mid-2000s R-rated comedy boom that produced hits like The Hangover (2009) and Step Brothers (2008). However, it relies too heavily on shock value and crude humor without the character development or memorable set pieces that made those films successful. Its approach to sales comedy feels dated compared to predecessors like Tin Men (1987).
While the film's portrayal of aggressive sales tactics and economic desperation could resonate in today's gig economy, its execution lacks the insight or wit to make meaningful commentary. Modern audiences might find its humor style dated and its treatment of certain subjects problematic compared to more nuanced contemporary comedies like The Big Short (2015).
