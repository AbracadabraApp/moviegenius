Set in an all-girls school in Thailand, 'Glee' follows Sai, a talented choir student who becomes the target of relentless bullying after a video of her goes viral. The film masterfully weaves supernatural horror elements with a stark examination of cyber-bullying and teenage social dynamics. As the harassment escalates, the line between psychological trauma and supernatural revenge becomes increasingly blurred, creating a haunting meditation on modern teenage life.
Pattarasaya Kreursuwansiri delivers a devastating performance as Sai, capturing both vulnerability and mounting darkness. Director Anucha Boonyawatana crafts a visually distinctive horror experience that owes as much to Carrie (1976) as it does to modern Asian horror classics like The Ring (1998). The school's oppressive atmosphere becomes a character itself, reminiscent of the institutional dread in Suspiria (2018).
The film presents a scathing critique of social media culture and institutional failure to protect vulnerable students. Like Eighth Grade (2018) and Cyberbully (2011), it captures the intense pressure of modern teenage life, but adds a supernatural element that serves as a metaphor for trauma and revenge. The traditional Thai ghost story elements are seamlessly integrated with contemporary fears about digital harassment.
While building on the foundation of Asian school-based horror films like Battle Royale (2000) and Death Bell (2008), 'Glee' brings a fresh perspective by focusing on the intersection of supernatural horror and digital age anxieties. The film's approach to vengeful spirits recalls Shutter (2004) while its examination of teenage cruelty echoes Mean Girls (2004), creating a unique hybrid that pushes both genres forward.
In an era where cyberbullying and social media pressure continue to affect young people globally, 'Glee' offers a powerful commentary on these issues through the lens of horror. Its themes of institutional negligence and collective guilt remain painfully relevant, while its supernatural elements serve as an effective metaphor for the very real horrors of teenage life in the digital age.
