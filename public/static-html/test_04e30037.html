Following the events of the first film, Ex-Deputy So & So (James Ransone) now works as a private investigator trying to stop Bughuul's cycle of violence by burning down houses where the murders occurred. He discovers Courtney Collins (Shannyn Sossamon) and her twin sons Dylan and Zach hiding from their abusive husband/father in one such house. Dylan is being visited by ghost children who show him disturbing murder films, attempting to convince him to kill his family and join their ranks. The sequel attempts to expand the mythology but loses the original's sense of discovery and dread in favor of more explicit supernatural elements.
James Ransone brings an earnest energy to his expanded role, but the film struggles to justify his character's promotion to protagonist. Shannyn Sossamon delivers a committed performance as the protective mother, though the domestic abuse subplot feels disconnected from the supernatural horror. Director Ciaran Foy, taking over from Scott Derrickson, favors a more conventional approach that lacks the original's methodical build-up and unsettling atmosphere.
While the film attempts to explore themes of generational trauma and child abuse, it never fully develops these ideas beyond surface-level plot devices. Unlike successful horror sequels like The Conjuring 2 (2016) or Ouija: Origin of Evil (2016) that found fresh angles on their premises, Sinister 2 retreads familiar ground without adding meaningful depth to its predecessor's mythology.
Released during a period of strong supernatural horror films like It Follows (2014) and The Babadook (2014), Sinister 2 falls into common sequel pitfalls by prioritizing explicit scares over psychological tension. It follows a pattern similar to Insidious: Chapter 2 (2013), where expanding the mythology comes at the cost of the original's effective simplicity.
The film's exploration of found footage and cursed media feels less relevant in an era where more innovative horror films like Host (2020) and Searching (2018) have found fresh ways to incorporate modern technology into their scares. Its conventional approach to supernatural horror appears dated compared to more recent genre-pushing works like Hereditary (2018) or Midsommar (2019).
