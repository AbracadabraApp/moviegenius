This modern reimagining of About Last Night (1986) follows two couples navigating the treacherous waters of modern dating in Los Angeles. Danny (Michael Ealy) and Debbie (Joy Bryant) represent the traditional romantic storyline, while Bernie (Kevin Hart) and Joan (Regina Hall) provide the explosive comedy counterpoint. What begins as casual hookups evolves into something deeper, forcing all four characters to confront their fears about commitment, vulnerability, and the risk of real intimacy.
Kevin Hart and Regina Hall steal every scene they're in, creating a dynamic that feels both hilariously over-the-top and surprisingly genuine. Their performances elevate what could have been standard romantic comedy fare into something more memorable. Michael Ealy and Joy Bryant provide the emotional anchor, delivering nuanced performances that explore the more serious aspects of modern relationships. Director Steve Pink smartly updates the material while maintaining the core emotional truth of David Mamet's original stage play 'Sexual Perversity in Chicago.'
The film offers a fresh perspective on dating in the social media age, where casual hookups and genuine connection often blur. Unlike many romantic comedies that shy away from sexual politics, this version tackles modern relationship dynamics head-on, similar to how Trainwreck (2015) and Crazy, Stupid, Love (2011) would approach the subject. The film's exploration of how friendships change when relationships get serious feels particularly authentic.
While the romantic comedy genre has evolved since classics like When Harry Met Sally (1989), About Last Night manages to bridge the gap between traditional rom-com storytelling and contemporary sensibilities. It shares DNA with films like Think Like a Man (2012) and The Best Man (1999) in its ability to balance relationship comedy with genuine emotional stakes.
The film's honest portrayal of relationship fears, commitment issues, and the challenge of maintaining individuality while building a partnership remains relevant. Like 500 Days of Summer (2009), it acknowledges that modern relationships don't always follow traditional paths, making it resonate with contemporary audiences navigating similar waters.
