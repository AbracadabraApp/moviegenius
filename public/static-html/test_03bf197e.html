Post Tenebras Lux follows Juan and Natalia, an upper-middle-class couple living in rural Mexico with their young children. Through a series of non-linear, sometimes dreamlike sequences, we witness their struggles with marriage, class differences, and existential questions. The film opens with an extraordinary sequence of a young child wandering among farm animals at dusk, setting the tone for its experimental nature. The narrative weaves between reality and dreams, including surreal encounters with a glowing red devil figure and scenes from an English rugby match that seem disconnected yet resonate thematically.
Reygadas directs with bold artistic choices, most notably using a custom-made lens that creates prismatic distortions around the frame edges, literally blurring the line between reality and dreams. The performances are raw and naturalistic, with non-professional actors bringing authenticity to their roles. The director's vision recalls the spiritual cinema of The Tree of Life (2011) and Uncle Boonmee Who Can Recall His Past Lives (2010), yet forges its own unique visual language.
The film masterfully explores class tensions in contemporary Mexico, drawing parallels with works like Roma (2018) and Y Tu Mamá También (2001). Through Juan's relationship with his workers and the local community, Reygadas examines privilege, guilt, and social responsibility. The film's treatment of violence and sexuality recalls the unflinching gaze of Irreversible (2002), while maintaining its own meditative pace.
Within experimental cinema, Post Tenebras Lux stands alongside works like Enter the Void (2009) and The Holy Mountain (1973) in its willingness to break conventional narrative structures. Its innovative use of light and natural elements echoes Terrence Malick's later works, while its surreal elements recall the dream logic of Mulholland Drive (2001).
The film's exploration of class inequality, environmental concerns, and spiritual searching remains deeply relevant. Its visual experimentation has influenced contemporary art house cinema, seen in works like The Green Knight (2021) and Memoria (2021). The film's meditation on family and nature speaks to modern anxieties about connection and environmental degradation.
