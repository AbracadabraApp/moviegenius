This groundbreaking documentary brings together three iconic guitarists from different generations: Jimmy Page (Led Zeppelin), The Edge (U2), and Jack White (The White Stripes). The film masterfully weaves between their individual stories and a summit where they share techniques, memories, and passion for their craft. Each artist represents a distinct approach to the electric guitar, from Page's blues-based virtuosity to The Edge's effects-driven soundscapes to White's raw, primitive energy.
Director Davis Guggenheim, fresh from An Inconvenient Truth (2006), approaches these artists with reverence but maintains enough distance to let their personalities shine through naturally. The film's genius lies in how it captures intimate moments of creation and reflection. Watching Page air-guitar to Link Wray's Rumble (1958), or The Edge breaking down the bare bones of 'Where the Streets Have No Name,' offers unprecedented insight into their creative processes.
The documentary arrives at a crucial moment in music history, as digital production threatens to overshadow traditional instrumentation. It makes a compelling case for the electric guitar's continued relevance, much like Sound City (2013) would later do for analog recording. The film demonstrates how each generation redefines and revolutionizes the instrument, from Page's session musician days to The Edge's technological innovations to White's back-to-basics approach.
Unlike traditional music documentaries like The Last Waltz (1978) or Don't Look Back (1967), this film focuses on the instrument itself rather than band dynamics or tour life. It shares more DNA with intimate artist studies like Searching for Sugar Man (2012) and 20 Feet from Stardom (2013), prioritizing the creative process over celebrity.
In an era of bedroom producers and digital instruments, the film celebrates the raw power of electric guitar while acknowledging its evolution. It resonates with similar explorations of artistic craft like Jiro Dreams of Sushi (2011) and Side by Side (2012), asking vital questions about tradition, innovation, and authenticity in art.
