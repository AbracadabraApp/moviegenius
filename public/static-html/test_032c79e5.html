Gettin' Square follows Barry 'Wattsy' Wirth, a reformed criminal trying to go straight after prison, alongside his drug-addicted friend Johnny Spitieri. Set against the glitzy backdrop of Queensland's Gold Coast, the film masterfully balances crime thriller elements with comedy as Barry attempts to establish a legitimate restaurant business while crooked cops and old criminal associates threaten to drag him back into the underworld. The story's strength lies in its ability to make viewers genuinely care about these flawed characters while maintaining a sharp comic edge.
David Wenham delivers one of Australian cinema's most memorable performances as Johnny Spitieri, particularly in the hilarious courtroom scene that has become legendary. His portrayal of the heroin-addicted small-time criminal manages to be both comedic and deeply human. Sam Worthington brings a sturdy presence as Barry, while director Jonathan Teplitzky maintains perfect tonal balance between the film's darker elements and its comedy, reminiscent of the way Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels (1998) handled similar material.
The film offers a uniquely Australian take on the crime comedy genre, exploring themes of redemption and loyalty against the backdrop of Gold Coast's criminal underbelly. It captures the specific cultural moment of early 2000s Australia, particularly the contrast between the glossy tourist facade and the grittier reality beneath. The film's exploration of corruption and the difficulty of escaping one's past resonates with similar themes in Animal Kingdom (2010) and Chopper (2000).
Gettin' Square stands out in the Australian crime genre by successfully mixing genuine tension with laugh-out-loud comedy, following in the tradition of Two Hands (1999) while carving its own distinct path. It balances the grittiness of films like The Hard Word (2002) with moments of pure comedy that never undermine the stakes of the story.
The film's themes of institutional corruption and the challenges faced by those trying to reform remain relevant today. Its exploration of friendship and loyalty under pressure continues to resonate, while its unique blend of comedy and crime has influenced later Australian films like The Square (2008) and Son of a Gun (2014).
