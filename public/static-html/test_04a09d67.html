RocknRolla returns to Guy Ritchie's beloved London criminal underworld, where old-school gangster Lenny Cole rules through property scams and political manipulation. When a wealthy Russian property dealer enters the scene, it triggers a complex web of double-crosses involving Cole's stepson Johnny Quid, a presumed-dead rock star, small-time crooks One Two and Mumbles, and a painting that everyone seems to want. The plot spirals with Ritchie's trademark complexity, weaving together criminal enterprises, real estate deals, and rock and roll excess.
Tom Wilkinson brings gravitas as the menacing Lenny Cole, while Gerard Butler and Idris Elba shine as the charming criminals One Two and Mumbles. Thandiwe Newton adds sophisticated intrigue as the manipulative accountant Stella. Toby Kebbell steals scenes as the unpredictable Johnny Quid, bringing both danger and dark humor to every moment on screen. Ritchie's direction maintains his signature kinetic energy while maturing beyond the style of his earlier works.
The film captures London at a crucial moment of transformation, where old criminal empires clash with new money and international influence. It explores themes of loyalty, tradition versus progress, and the changing face of organized crime in a globalized world. The movie's examination of Russian oligarch influence in London proved remarkably prescient for its time.
RocknRolla builds on the British gangster film renaissance that Ritchie helped establish with Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels (1998) and Snatch (2000). While it shares DNA with these predecessors, it brings a more sophisticated edge to the genre, incorporating elements of international crime thrillers and social commentary while maintaining the quick-witted humor and intricate plotting that fans expect.
The themes of foreign money influencing local power structures and the intersection of crime with legitimate business remain startlingly relevant. The film's style continues to influence modern crime comedies, while its observations about London's transformation have only grown more pertinent with time.
