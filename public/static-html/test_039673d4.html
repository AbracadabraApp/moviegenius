Supergirl follows Kara Zor-El (Helen Slater), Superman's cousin, who journeys to Earth from the surviving Kryptonian city of Argo City in pursuit of a powerful energy source called the Omegahedron. Landing in small-town America, she assumes the identity of Linda Lee while searching for the device, which has fallen into the hands of wannabe witch Selena (Faye Dunaway). The film attempts to establish its own identity separate from the Christopher Reeve Superman films, but struggles with an unfocused narrative that can't decide if it's targeting children or adults.
Helen Slater brings an earnest charm to Kara/Supergirl, but the material rarely allows her to soar. Faye Dunaway camps it up as the villainous Selena, channeling a performance somewhere between her work in Mommie Dearest (1981) and the kind of theatrical villainy Michelle Pfeiffer would later perfect in Batman Returns (1992). Peter O'Toole appears briefly as Zaltar, lending gravitas to an otherwise lightweight production, but even his considerable talents can't elevate the muddled screenplay.
Released during a period when female-led action films were rare, Supergirl represented a missed opportunity to prove the viability of female superhero movies. Its failure would unfortunately be used as industry evidence against such projects for decades, until films like Wonder Woman (2017) and Captain Marvel (2019) finally broke the barrier. The film's treatment of its female protagonist reflects 1980s gender politics, with an awkward mix of empowerment and stereotypical 'feminine' concerns.
Coming after the decline of the Superman franchise with Superman III (1983), Supergirl attempted to expand the DC cinematic universe before such concepts were common. Its struggles presaged later superhero spin-off difficulties like Catwoman (2004) and Elektra (2005). The film's effects and tone feel caught between the sincerity of Superman (1978) and the camp of Flash Gordon (1980), never successfully finding its own voice.
While dated in execution, Supergirl's existence as an early female superhero film makes it an interesting historical artifact in the evolution of the genre. Its mistakes helped inform the more successful approach of modern superhero films like Black Widow (2021) and the CW's Supergirl television series. The film's ambitious but flawed attempt to create a female superhero mythology independent of its male counterpart remains relevant to ongoing discussions about gender representation in superhero media.
