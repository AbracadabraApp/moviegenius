Following the events of the first film, Species II introduces astronaut Patrick Ross, who becomes infected with alien DNA during a Mars mission. Upon returning to Earth, he begins spreading the alien genetic material through a series of encounters, creating a new threat of hybrid offspring. The original film's survivors, Press Lennox (Michael Madsen) and Dr. Laura Baker (Marg Helgenberger), return to combat this new menace, aided by a more docile clone of Sil named Eve (Natasha Henstridge).
The returning cast members seem less engaged than in the original, with Madsen particularly appearing to phone in his performance. Henstridge, despite limited screen time, brings some nuance to her new role as Eve, though the character is underutilized. Director Peter Medak, known for more sophisticated horror like The Changeling (1980), struggles to balance the film's exploitation elements with genuine tension.
Released in the late 1990s amid a wave of science fiction sequels, Species II represents the era's declining standards for franchise continuations. While the original film touched on interesting themes about genetic engineering and biological imperatives, the sequel abandons most meaningful subtext in favor of shock value. It shares DNA with contemporary releases like Mimic (1997) and Deep Rising (1998) in its attempt to blend creature features with increasing levels of gore and sexuality.
The film sits awkwardly between the practical effects-driven horror of the early 1990s and the coming CGI revolution, never quite mastering either approach. Unlike successful sci-fi horror hybrids like Alien Resurrection (1997) or Deep Blue Sea (1999), Species II fails to evolve the formula established by its predecessor.
Species II serves as a cautionary tale about rushing sequels without strong creative direction. Its treatment of female characters and reliance on shock tactics feel particularly dated today, especially compared to modern sci-fi horror films like Annihilation (2018) that handle similar themes with more sophistication and depth.
