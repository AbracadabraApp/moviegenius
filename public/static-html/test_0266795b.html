Ready, Fire, Aim examines the volatile world of Silicon Valley startups during the early 2000s, following three ambitious entrepreneurs attempting to launch their companies in the aftermath of the dot-com bubble. The documentary captures their struggles with venture capital funding, product development, and personal sacrifices, though often loses focus by trying to cover too many parallel storylines.
Director Chi Chi Navarro employs a direct cinema approach, attempting to capture authentic moments without intervention. While this creates some genuine insights into startup culture, the film suffers from inconsistent pacing and missed opportunities to dig deeper into crucial moments of decision-making and conflict.
The film provides a time capsule of early 2000s tech culture, capturing both the lingering optimism and growing skepticism following the dot-com crash. However, its observations feel superficial compared to later works like The Social Network (2010) and Startup.com (2001), which offer more nuanced examinations of tech entrepreneurship.
As a business documentary, Ready, Fire, Aim falls into the observational tradition of films like Inside Job (2010) and Enron: The Smartest Guys in the Room (2005), but lacks their investigative depth and narrative cohesion. The film's scattered approach diminishes its potential impact on the genre.
While startup culture remains relevant today, the film's dated perspective and surface-level analysis limit its current value. Modern viewers would find more insight in recent works like WeWork: Or the Making and Breaking of a $47 Billion Unicorn (2021) or The Inventor: Out for Blood in Silicon Valley (2019).
