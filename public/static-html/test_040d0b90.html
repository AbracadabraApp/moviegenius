The Last Kiss (L'Ultimo Bacio) follows Carlo, a 29-year-old architect facing an existential crisis as his girlfriend Giulia announces her pregnancy. The news triggers a cascade of emotional turmoil as Carlo grapples with commitment fears and an attraction to a younger woman, while his friends navigate their own relationship challenges. The film masterfully weaves multiple character arcs exploring love, betrayal, and the fear of growing older, creating a tapestry of modern relationships reminiscent of Singles (1992) but with distinctly Italian passion.
Director Gabriele Muccino crafts an intimate portrayal of millennial anxiety with kinetic camera work that mirrors the characters' emotional turbulence. Stefano Accorsi delivers a compelling performance as Carlo, balancing charm with selfishness in ways that echo similar character studies in High Fidelity (2000). The ensemble cast creates authentic chemistry that elevates the film beyond typical relationship drama territory.
Released at the turn of the millennium, the film captures a generation's struggle with commitment and adulthood, predating similar themes in Garden State (2004) and 500 Days of Summer (2009). It examines how traditional Italian family values clash with modern relationships, while exploring universal themes of marriage, fidelity, and personal growth that would later influence films like Blue Valentine (2010).
The film reinvigorated the Italian romantic drama genre, bringing a raw authenticity that influenced both European cinema and Hollywood, leading to an American remake in 2006. Its style bridges the gap between classic Italian romance like Cinema Paradiso (1988) and contemporary relationship studies like Marriage Story (2019), offering a blueprint for honest portrayal of modern love's complexities.
Twenty years later, the film's exploration of commitment phobia, quarter-life crisis, and the tension between personal freedom and responsibility remains startlingly relevant. Its influence can be seen in recent works like Normal People (2020) and Malcolm & Marie (2021), proving its themes continue to resonate with new generations facing similar life transitions.
