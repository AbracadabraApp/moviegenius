Set on a stunning Mediterranean island resort, this elegant Agatha Christie adaptation follows Belgian detective Hercule Poirot investigating the murder of a famous stage actress. When beautiful but manipulative Arlena Marshall is found strangled on a secluded beach, everyone at the exclusive hotel becomes a suspect. The brilliance lies in how the story weaves together multiple character motivations, carefully planted clues, and precise timing to create an intricate puzzle that only Poirot's 'little grey cells' can unravel.
Peter Ustinov delivers his third and most assured performance as Poirot, bringing warmth and wit to Christie's famous detective. The ensemble cast sparkles, particularly Diana Rigg as the doomed actress and Maggie Smith as a sharp-tongued hotel manager. Director Guy Hamilton, fresh from his James Bond successes, brings a polished style that perfectly captures the glamorous 1930s setting while maintaining narrative tension.
The film exemplifies the enduring appeal of the classic British murder mystery, following the success of Death on the Nile (1978) and Murder on the Orient Express (1974). It represents the height of lavish Christie adaptations before the genre shifted toward grittier crime dramas in the late 1980s. The story's exploration of celebrity culture, jealousy, and appearance versus reality remains remarkably relevant.
While following Christie's tried-and-true formula, the film distinguishes itself through its sophisticated humor and self-awareness. It builds on the tradition of The Last of Sheila (1973) and influences later ensemble mysteries like Gosford Park (2001). The careful balance of comedy and suspense would later inspire films like Knives Out (2019).
Modern audiences continue to appreciate the film's craftsmanship, wit, and escapist appeal. Its influence can be seen in recent Christie adaptations like Murder on the Orient Express (2017) and Death on the Nile (2022), though few match its perfect blend of sophistication and entertainment.
