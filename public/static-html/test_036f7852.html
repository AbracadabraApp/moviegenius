Lieutenant Frank Drebin returns in this sequel that somehow manages to be as absurdly entertaining as its predecessor. The plot, involving an energy industry conspiracy, serves merely as a clothesline to hang an endless array of sight gags, puns, and physical comedy. Nielsen's Drebin remains the heart of the film, delivering ridiculous lines with unwavering seriousness while stumbling through increasingly preposterous situations with deadpan grace.
Leslie Nielsen's evolution from dramatic actor to comedy icon reaches its zenith here, perfecting the straight-faced delivery that made him the master of spoof comedy. The ensemble cast, including Priscilla Presley and George Kennedy, maintains the crucial balance of playing it completely straight while surrounded by absolute chaos. Director David Zucker orchestrates the mayhem with precision timing, understanding that successful parody requires meticulous attention to detail.
While primarily focused on laughs, the film cleverly satirizes early 90s environmental politics and energy policy debates, wrapping serious topics in layers of absurdist humor. The approach follows in the tradition of Dr. Strangelove (1964), using comedy to tackle contemporary issues while never letting the message overshadow the entertainment.
Building on the foundation laid by Airplane! (1980), this sequel represents the zenith of the ZAZ (Zucker, Abrahams, Zucker) style of comedy. Its influence can be seen in everything from Hot Shots! (1991) to Scary Movie (1999), though few achieved the same level of consistent quality. The film demonstrates how spoof comedy can work when treated with genuine craft rather than just throwing random references at the screen.
In an era of increasingly self-aware comedy, The Naked Gun 2½'s commitment to playing it straight while being absolutely ridiculous feels refreshingly pure. Its influence can be seen in modern works like 21 Jump Street (2012) and MacGruber (2010), though few modern comedies match its density of jokes or hit rate.
