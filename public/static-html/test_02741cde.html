Set in the ancient holy city of Varanasi, this thriller follows British teacher Helen (Holly Gilbert) who travels to India seeking answers about her sister's death. She becomes entangled in a series of ritualistic murders being investigated by Inspector Nair (Adil Hussain). The film attempts to weave together elements of spiritual mysticism with crime thriller conventions, using the atmospheric backdrop of Varanasi's ghats and temples to create an sense of unease and cultural displacement.
Adil Hussain delivers the film's strongest performance as the world-weary inspector, bringing nuance to what could have been a standard procedural role. However, the supporting cast struggles with uneven material. First-time director Rajan Kumar Patel shows promise in his visual approach, particularly in capturing Varanasi's ethereal quality, but the pacing feels hesitant and uncertain. The film's atmosphere recalls elements of Slumdog Millionaire (2008) and The Namesake (2006), though without achieving their narrative cohesion.
The film attempts to explore the collision between Western perspectives and Eastern spirituality, similar to The Best Exotic Marigold Hotel (2011), but with darker undertones. While it raises interesting questions about cultural appropriation and the Western gaze on Indian traditions, these themes remain largely surface-level. The depiction of Varanasi's religious practices and death rituals aims for authenticity but occasionally falls into exoticization.
As a thriller set in India, the film follows in the footsteps of works like Sacred (2016) and Monsoon (2014), attempting to blend crime genre elements with cultural exploration. However, it struggles to balance its multiple ambitions, never fully committing to either the thriller aspects or the deeper cultural examination it hints at.
While the film's exploration of cross-cultural understanding remains relevant, its execution feels dated compared to more nuanced recent works like The White Tiger (2021) or Lion (2016). Its treatment of spiritual practices and murder investigation fails to reach the sophisticated integration achieved by similar contemporary films.
