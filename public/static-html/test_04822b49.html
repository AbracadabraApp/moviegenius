Set two years after the events of the legendary video game, 'Final Fantasy VII: Advent Children' follows Cloud Strife, still haunted by past failures, as he confronts a mysterious illness spreading through the city of Edge. When three silver-haired men appear seeking their 'mother' Jenova, Cloud must overcome his emotional isolation to protect a new generation of children, including Marlene and Denzel. The film masterfully balances fan service with newcomer accessibility, though it clearly rewards those familiar with the source material.
Director Tetsuya Nomura delivers a visual feast that set new standards for CGI anime features, comparable to the impact of Ghost in the Shell (1995) and Akira (1988). The voice performances, both in Japanese and English, bring emotional depth to characters fans had only seen in polygonal form. The film's approach to realistic human animation influenced later works like Kingsglaive: Final Fantasy XV (2016) and Detective Pikachu (2019).
The film explores themes of environmental destruction, corporate responsibility, and personal redemption that resonate with contemporary audiences. Like Princess Mononoke (1997), it tackles ecological messages within its fantasy framework. The portrayal of grief, guilt, and healing speaks to post-traumatic growth, while the community's response to widespread illness feels particularly relevant in our post-pandemic world.
As a video game adaptation, 'Advent Children' raised the bar for the genre, proving that game narratives could be successfully translated to film while maintaining their emotional core. It paved the way for more ambitious game adaptations like Warcraft (2016) and Sonic the Hedgehog (2020), while its action choreography influenced films like Sucker Punch (2011).
The film's themes of healing from collective trauma and rebuilding society remain deeply relevant. Its visual style continues to influence modern animation and live-action films, particularly in how they approach fantasy combat sequences. The attention to character psychology and emotional storytelling set standards that recent game adaptations like The Last of Us (2023) series continue to follow.
