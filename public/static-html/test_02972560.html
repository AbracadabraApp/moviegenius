The First Power

Detective Russell Logan (Lou Diamond Phillips) tracks down a satanic serial killer Patrick Channing, who is executed only to return from death with supernatural powers. The film blends police procedural elements with supernatural horror as Logan teams with psychic Tess Seaton to stop the seemingly unstoppable killer. While the premise echoes elements of The Hidden (1987) and Fallen (1998), it never quite achieves their narrative sophistication or atmospheric tension.
Lou Diamond Phillips delivers a committed performance, though the material often leaves him stranded in generic action-hero territory. Jeff Kober brings genuine menace to the role of Channing, creating some effectively disturbing moments. Director Robert Resnikoff attempts to merge the gritty police thriller aesthetics of Se7en (1995) with supernatural horror elements, but the execution feels more aligned with direct-to-video fare of the era.
Released during the tail end of the 1980s Satanic Panic, the film reflects cultural anxieties about occult influences and religious faith. It attempts to tackle themes of good versus evil and the nature of belief, but never delves deep enough to make meaningful statements. The film's treatment of supernatural elements feels derivative of stronger entries like The Exorcist (1973) and Prince of Darkness (1987).
The First Power represents a transitional period in horror cinema, bridging the supernatural slashers of the 1980s with the more psychological thrillers that would dominate the 1990s. While it predates the religious horror revival led by The Prophecy (1995) and Stigmata (1999), it lacks their theological complexity and visual sophistication.
The film's blend of detective work and supernatural elements prefigures later works like Constantine (2005) and Deliver Us from Evil (2014), though its execution feels dated. Its themes of faith versus skepticism and the nature of evil remain relevant, but modern audiences may find its approach simplistic compared to contemporary genre offerings.
