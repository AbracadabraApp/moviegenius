A Thousand Cuts follows Maria Ressa, CEO of Philippine news site Rappler, as she fights for press freedom under President Rodrigo Duterte's increasingly authoritarian regime. The documentary captures Ressa's unwavering determination as she faces multiple arrest warrants, online harassment, and government intimidation while trying to maintain independent journalism. Through intimate access, we witness her navigate both personal threats and institutional challenges, revealing the human cost of speaking truth to power.
Director Ramona S. Diaz crafts a masterful vérité style that creates immediate urgency, following Ressa with remarkable intimacy during crucial moments. The film's strength lies in its ability to capture real-time history through Ressa's perspective, reminiscent of the journalistic dedication seen in Spotlight (2015) and the political tension of The Post (2017). The documentary's pacing maintains suspense while clearly explaining complex political dynamics.
The film serves as a crucial warning about the fragility of democracy and the global rise of authoritarianism. Like Collective (2019) and Citizenfour (2014), it demonstrates how independent journalism becomes a critical battlefield in the fight for democratic values. The documentary expertly shows how social media can be weaponized against truth, creating parallels with issues explored in The Social Dilemma (2020) and The Great Hack (2019).
Within the political documentary genre, A Thousand Cuts stands alongside powerful works like All the President's Men (1976) and The Act of Killing (2012) in its examination of power, truth, and accountability. The film distinguishes itself by capturing events as they unfold, creating an unprecedented level of immediacy and stakes reminiscent of 5 Broken Cameras (2011).
The film's examination of disinformation campaigns, online harassment, and threats to democracy remains incredibly relevant, especially as similar patterns emerge globally. Its warnings about the manipulation of social media and the erosion of press freedom echo contemporary concerns addressed in Don't Look Up (2021) and The Trial of the Chicago 7 (2020), making it essential viewing for understanding current global political dynamics.
