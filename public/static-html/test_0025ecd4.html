Following dentist McTeague's descent into moral corruption after his wife Trina wins a lottery prize, 'Greed' presents an unflinching look at how money destroys relationships and human dignity. The film's raw psychological realism was unprecedented for its era, with von Stroheim refusing to romanticize his characters or provide moral comfort. The tragic spiral of McTeague and Trina's relationship remains shocking even by today's standards, building to one of cinema's most devastating conclusions in Death Valley.
Von Stroheim's insistence on location shooting and naturalistic performances created a new template for cinematic realism. Gibson Gowland and ZaSu Pitts deliver startlingly modern performances, eschewing the melodramatic style common in silent films. The director's legendary original 8-hour cut, though lost, represents one of cinema's great 'what-if' scenarios. Even in its truncated form, the film's influence on neorealism and psychological drama cannot be overstated.
Released during the Roaring Twenties' excess, 'Greed' offered a prescient critique of American materialism and class anxiety. Its unflinching examination of moral corruption through wealth anticipates both the Great Depression and later works like Citizen Kane (1941) and There Will Be Blood (2007). The film's themes of capitalism's dehumanizing effects remain painfully relevant today.
While most silent dramas relied on melodramatic conventions, 'Greed' pioneered psychological realism that wouldn't become mainstream until decades later. Its influence can be traced through films like The Treasure of the Sierra Madre (1948) and Raging Bull (1980), establishing a template for character-driven tragedy that emphasizes psychological deterioration over external conflict.
Modern filmmakers continue drawing inspiration from 'Greed's' uncompromising vision. Its DNA is visible in works like Breaking Bad (2008-2013) and Parasite (2019), which similarly explore how money corrupts human relationships. The film's technical innovations in location shooting and natural lighting influenced the entire Italian Neorealist movement and continue inspiring directors seeking authenticity.
