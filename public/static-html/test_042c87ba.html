This unique music video documentary presents two distinct versions of Metallica's groundbreaking song 'One.' The release compares the original concept version incorporating footage from the 1971 film 'Johnny Got His Gun' with a performance-only version. The documentary provides fascinating insight into how different visual approaches can dramatically alter the impact of the same musical piece.
Director Wayne Isham, known for his work with Mötley Crüe and Def Leppard, brings a cinematic quality to both versions. The band's performance footage showcases Metallica at their technical peak, with James Hetfield's intense vocals and Kirk Hammett's legendary guitar work captured in stark black and white cinematography. The integration of film footage in the first version creates a haunting anti-war narrative that elevates the song's message.
Released during the MTV era's golden age, '2 of One' represents a crucial moment when metal bands began taking music videos seriously as an art form. The documentary format was revolutionary for its time, predating behind-the-scenes features that would become standard on later DVD releases. It showed how metal could tackle serious themes like war and mental trauma through visual storytelling.
This release helped legitimize metal music videos beyond the typical performance footage approach seen in works like Quiet Riot: Bang Your Head (1983). It influenced later metal documentaries like Some Kind of Monster (2004) and set new standards for how heavy music could be presented visually.
The dual-version concept remains influential in modern music video production, where artists often release multiple cuts of videos. Its exploration of war themes through music video continues to resonate, especially in an era of renewed global conflicts and media manipulation.
