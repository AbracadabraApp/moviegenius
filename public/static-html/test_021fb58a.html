Striptease follows Erin Grant, a former FBI clerk who turns to exotic dancing to fund a custody battle for her daughter. What could have been a sharp commentary on politics and desperation in Florida becomes a muddled attempt at both comedy and drama. The film struggles to balance its serious custody plot with broad comedy involving corrupt congressman David Dilbeck's obsession with Erin. Despite Demi Moore's committed performance and a $12.5 million payday, the character never fully develops beyond surface-level motivation.
Moore approaches the role with dedication, having trained extensively and achieving impressive physical transformation. However, the film's uneven tone undermines her efforts. Burt Reynolds plays Congressman Dilbeck as an over-the-top caricature, while Ving Rhames provides the few genuine moments of warmth as Erin's protective bouncer. Director Andrew Bergman, known for The Freshman (1990), fails to recapture the satirical brilliance of his earlier work.
Released in the mid-90s, Striptease arrived during a period of increasing mainstream fascination with adult entertainment, following films like Showgirls (1995). Unlike The Full Monty (1997), which successfully explored economic desperation through adult entertainment with heart and humor, Striptease never commits to either serious commentary or effective satire. The film's attempts to address serious issues like custody rights and political corruption get lost in its tonal confusion.
The film attempts to blend elements of social satire, crime comedy, and drama in the vein of Erin Brockovich (2000), but lacks the latter's focused narrative and clear message. Its Florida setting and political corruption themes could have tapped into the same vein as Miami Blues (1990), but instead delivers neither compelling drama nor effective comedy.
While themes of economic inequality and women's autonomy remain relevant, modern films like Hustlers (2019) handle similar subject matter with far more nuance and cultural awareness. Striptease's dated approach to its subject matter and confused messaging make it more of a cultural curiosity than a meaningful commentary on its themes.
