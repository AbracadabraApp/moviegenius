Through Black Spruce follows Annie Bird's search for her missing sister Suzanne in Toronto while their uncle Will deals with escalating tensions back home in Moosonee. The parallel narratives explore indigenous identity, family bonds, and the crisis of missing indigenous women in Canada. The story attempts to bridge urban and traditional Native life, showing Annie navigating the fashion world while Will confronts local drug dealers and his own demons.
Tanaya Beatty delivers a committed performance as Annie, though the script often leaves her character's emotional journey underexplored. Brandon Oakes brings quiet dignity to Will, creating the film's most compelling character. Don McKellar's direction, while respectful, maintains a distance from the material that sometimes feels too cautious, preventing the story from achieving its full dramatic potential.
The film tackles the crucial issue of missing and murdered indigenous women in Canada, following in the footsteps of films like Wind River (2017) and Frozen River (2008). While its intentions are noble, the execution falls short of fully illuminating this crisis. The dual exploration of traditional versus modern indigenous life recalls themes from Smoke Signals (1998), though with less narrative cohesion.
Within the landscape of indigenous cinema, Through Black Spruce attempts to bridge social issues drama with mystery elements, similar to Mystery Road (2013). However, it struggles to achieve the raw power of films like Once Were Warriors (1994) or the cultural insight of Atanarjuat: The Fast Runner (2001). The film's approach to contemporary indigenous issues feels more tentative than groundbreaking.
The film's examination of missing indigenous women remains painfully relevant, though recent works like Blood Quantum (2019) and Night Raiders (2021) have approached indigenous stories with more innovative and impactful storytelling. The tension between tradition and modernity continues to resonate, but the film's measured approach sometimes dulls its urgent message.
