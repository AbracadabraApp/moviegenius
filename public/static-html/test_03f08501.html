Ashanti follows Dr. David Linderby (Michael Caine) on a desperate search across Africa when his wife Anansa (Beverly Johnson) is kidnapped by slave traders. The film attempts to shed light on modern slavery while wrapping it in the framework of an adventure thriller. Despite its serious subject matter, the execution often feels more exploitative than enlightening. Peter Ustinov's portrayal of the slave trader Suleiman creates an interesting antagonist, but the character development remains surface-level throughout.
Richard Fleischer, known for films like 20,000 Leagues Under the Sea (1954) and Soylent Green (1973), brings his action-adventure expertise but struggles to balance the entertainment aspects with the grave subject matter. Michael Caine delivers a committed performance, though he seems constrained by the uneven script. Former model Beverly Johnson's film debut shows promise but suffers from limited screen time and development.
Released in 1979, Ashanti attempted to address the ongoing issue of human trafficking in Africa, predating later films like Trade (2007) and Taken (2008). While its intentions were noble, the film's approach now feels dated and occasionally tone-deaf. The movie sits uncomfortably between serious drama and exploitation cinema, never fully succeeding as either.
The film emerged during a period when international adventure films like The Wild Geese (1978) and The Dogs of War (1980) were popular. However, Ashanti lacks the political complexity of these contemporaries. Its treatment of African culture and issues feels superficial compared to more nuanced works like Zulu (1964) or even Live and Let Die (1973).
While human trafficking remains a pressing global issue, modern films like Blood Diamond (2006) and Sicario (2015) handle similar themes with greater sophistication and impact. Ashanti's dated portrayal of Africa and its people makes it more of a historical curiosity than a meaningful commentary on contemporary problems.
