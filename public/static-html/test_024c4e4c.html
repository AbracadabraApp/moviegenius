Like Stars on Earth follows Ishaan Awasthi, a 9-year-old boy struggling with dyslexia in an education system that doesn't understand him. Through vibrant imagination sequences and heartbreaking reality checks, we experience his world of confused letters, artistic talent, and growing isolation. When art teacher Ram Shankar Nikumbh enters his life, everything changes. The film masterfully balances Ishaan's internal struggles with the external pressures of academic performance and parental expectations, creating a narrative that's both intimately personal and universally relatable.
Darsheel Safary delivers a breakthrough performance that ranks among the best child acting in cinema, reminiscent of Haley Joel Osment in The Sixth Sense (1999). Aamir Khan's direction shows remarkable restraint, allowing the story to unfold naturally while using animation and artistic sequences to visualize Ishaan's unique perspective. The approach echoes the imaginative elements of Pan's Labyrinth (2006), where fantasy becomes a coping mechanism for reality.
The film revolutionized conversations about learning disabilities in Indian cinema, much like Rain Man (1988) did for autism awareness. It challenges traditional educational paradigms while celebrating different forms of intelligence and creativity. The impact mirrors that of Dead Poets Society (1989) in its critique of rigid educational systems and celebration of individual potential.
Within the genre of educational drama, Like Stars on Earth stands alongside Freedom Writers (2007) and Stand and Deliver (1988) in depicting teachers who transform students' lives. However, it distinguishes itself through its immersive portrayal of dyslexia and its integration of animation to represent a child's worldview, similar to the innovative storytelling in Inside Out (2015).
The film's messages about neurodiversity and educational reform remain crucial today, especially as global education systems grapple with inclusive learning. Its influence can be seen in later works like Wonder (2017) and continues to inspire discussions about alternative teaching methods and the importance of recognizing different types of intelligence.
