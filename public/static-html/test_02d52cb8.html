Kenneth Branagh returns as Hercule Poirot in this lavish but ultimately hollow adaptation of Agatha Christie's beloved mystery. The story follows the brilliant detective aboard a glamorous river steamer in Egypt, where the honeymoon of wealthy heiress Linnet Ridgeway-Doyle turns deadly. When a passenger is found murdered, Poirot must navigate through a web of jealousy, betrayal, and vengeance to find the killer among the boat's privileged passengers. While the setup promises intrigue, the execution often favors style over substance, losing Christie's careful character development in favor of glossy production values.
Branagh's interpretation of Poirot, while competent, lacks the subtle eccentricity that made David Suchet's portrayal in the television series so memorable. Gal Gadot and Armie Hammer as the central couple generate little chemistry, making their dramatic arc less compelling. The film's visual approach, while ambitious with its sweeping Egyptian vistas, relies too heavily on CGI, creating an artificial atmosphere that distances viewers from the period setting. This stands in stark contrast to the practical elegance of Evil Under the Sun (1982) and the original Death on the Nile (1978).
The film attempts to address colonialism and class dynamics in 1930s Egypt, but these themes feel superficial compared to modern mystery films like Gosford Park (2001) that more effectively tackle social hierarchies. The updated screenplay tries to inject contemporary sensibilities into Christie's work, but the changes often feel forced and disconnected from the source material's era-specific observations.
Within the recent revival of whodunits, including Knives Out (2019) and Murder on the Orient Express (2017), this adaptation struggles to find its footing. While these contemporary mysteries have successfully modernized the genre while maintaining tension and character depth, 'Death on the Nile' falls into the trap of prioritizing spectacle over the intricate puzzle-solving that makes Christie's works enduring classics.
The film's themes of obsessive love and wealth's corrupting influence remain relevant, but their exploration lacks the sophistication of modern mysteries like The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo (2011) or the sharp social commentary of Parasite (2019). While Christie's original story retains its power, this adaptation fails to make a compelling case for its necessity in today's mystery landscape.
