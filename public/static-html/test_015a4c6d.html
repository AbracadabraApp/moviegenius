Son of a Gun follows young JR (Brenton Thwaites), who enters prison and falls under the protection of Australia's most notorious criminal, Brendan Lynch (Ewan McGregor). Their relationship evolves from a prison alliance into a complex criminal partnership involving a high-stakes gold heist. The film masterfully builds tension as JR navigates between loyalty to his mentor and his growing feelings for Tasha (Alicia Vikander), while discovering that trust in the criminal world comes at a devastating price.
McGregor sheds his typically charming persona to deliver a ruthless, manipulative performance that recalls the intensity of Ben Kingsley in Sexy Beast (2000). First-time director Julius Avery brings a raw energy reminiscent of early Michael Mann, particularly Thief (1981), while maintaining distinctly Australian sensibilities. Thwaites holds his own against McGregor's commanding presence, creating a dynamic that echoes the mentor-protégé relationship in Animal Kingdom (2010).
The film explores themes of masculinity and power within Australia's criminal underworld, offering a fresh perspective on the crime genre through its examination of father-son dynamics and betrayal. Like The Place Beyond the Pines (2012), it delves into the cyclical nature of violence and the complex bonds between criminal fathers and their surrogate sons.
Within the crime thriller genre, Son of a Gun stands out for its uniquely Australian perspective, building on the gritty tradition of films like Chopper (2000) and Two Hands (1999). The heist elements recall Heat (1995), but with a more intimate focus on personal betrayal and mentorship gone wrong. The film's exploration of criminal relationships shares DNA with The Long Good Friday (1980), particularly in its examination of loyalty and power.
The film's themes of loyalty, betrayal, and the price of criminal ambition remain relevant in an era of increasing wealth inequality and moral ambiguity. Its exploration of father-son relationships and masculine identity politics resonates with contemporary discussions about toxic masculinity and generational trauma.
