First Love follows Elgin Smith, a college student who falls deeply in love with Caroline, a complex young woman with a troubled past. Set against the backdrop of 1970s campus life, the film attempts to capture the intensity and confusion of first romantic experiences. While the premise echoes elements of Love Story (1970), it lacks the emotional resonance that made that film a classic.
William Katt and Susan Dey deliver earnest but ultimately limited performances that reflect the film's superficial approach to young romance. The direction by Joan Darling, while competent, never fully explores the psychological depth possible in this coming-of-age narrative. John Barry's score provides some emotional weight, but can't compensate for the thin characterizations.
Released in the mid-1970s, First Love emerged during a period when American cinema was exploring more frank depictions of sexuality and relationships, following films like The Last Picture Show (1971) and Summer of '42 (1971). However, where those films broke new ground in their honest portrayal of young love, First Love feels more like a regression to safer, more conventional storytelling.
The college romance genre had evolved significantly by 1974, with films like The Way We Were (1973) setting new standards for romantic drama. First Love's conventional approach feels out of step with the era's more sophisticated relationship films, lacking the psychological complexity that audiences had come to expect following works like Five Easy Pieces (1970).
Modern viewers may find the film's portrayal of campus relationships and gender dynamics particularly dated. Unlike timeless coming-of-age stories such as The Graduate (1967) or even later works like Manhattan (1979), First Love hasn't aged well, offering little insight that resonates with contemporary audiences.
