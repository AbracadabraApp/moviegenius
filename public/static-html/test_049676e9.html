Kung Fury follows a Miami detective who gains kung fu powers after being struck by lightning and bitten by a cobra. When Adolf Hitler (the 'Kung Führer') time travels to Miami to steal his powers, our hero must journey through time to Nazi Germany to stop him. This deliberately absurd premise sets up a relentless parade of 1980s action movie tropes, including dinosaur-riding Vikings, sentient arcade machines, and a hacker who can 'hack time' using a Nintendo Power Glove.
David Sandberg's vision as both director and lead actor perfectly captures the earnest cheese of VHS-era action films. The intentionally hammy performances channel the spirit of movies like Big Trouble in Little China (1986) and Commando (1985), while the visual effects deliberately mimic the low-fi charm of straight-to-video releases. The film's commitment to its aesthetic extends to artificially added tracking errors and damage effects.
Released at the height of 1980s nostalgia, Kung Fury represents a watershed moment in crowdfunded filmmaking. Like Hobo with a Shotgun (2011), it evolved from a trailer into a full project through audience support. The film's viral success demonstrated how internet culture could resurrect and reinvent forgotten genres, paving the way for projects like Turbo Kid (2015).
Kung Fury exists in a lineage of loving genre parodies like Black Dynamite (2009) and Machete (2010). However, it pushes beyond simple mockery into a celebration of excess that recalls Dead or Alive (1999) and Danger 5 (2011). Its influence can be seen in later projects like Kung Pow: Enter the Fist (2002), showing how self-aware genre deconstruction became its own legitimate form.
The film's mashup of nostalgia, meme culture, and genuine filmmaking craft presaged the current era of self-referential content. Its success through crowdfunding and viral marketing demonstrated new possibilities for independent filmmaking. The upcoming feature-length sequel, starring Michael Fassbender and Arnold Schwarzenegger, proves the enduring appeal of its formula.
