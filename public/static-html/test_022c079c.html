Following the formula of creature features, this direct-to-video sequel follows Alexandra (Stephanie Niznik) and her husband Jason, who are rescued at sea by a mysterious vessel harboring dark secrets. The ship turns out to be a floating laboratory where mad scientist Dr. Grbac conducts genetic experiments creating human-spider hybrids. The plot borrows heavily from both Aliens (1986) and Deep Blue Sea (1999), but lacks their tension and production values.
The performances range from wooden to melodramatic, with even experienced TV actress Niznik struggling to sell the material. Director Sam Firstenberg, known for low-budget action films like American Ninja (1985), attempts to create claustrophobic tension in the ship's corridors but is hampered by budget constraints and dated CGI effects that make the spider creatures look particularly unconvincing.
Released in the early 2000s direct-to-video market, the film represents the tail end of the 1990s creature feature boom that followed the success of Anaconda (1997) and Deep Rising (1998). It demonstrates how digital effects, while more accessible, weren't yet sophisticated enough for lower-budget productions to create convincing monsters.
Within the spider horror subgenre, this sequel fails to reach the heights of classics like Eight Legged Freaks (2002) or the original Arachnophobia (1990). It follows the standard 'scientists playing god' narrative seen in better films like The Fly (1986) but lacks their philosophical depth or practical effects expertise.
The film's themes of genetic experimentation gone wrong remain relevant, but modern audiences accustomed to sophisticated effects in films like Life (2017) will find the dated CGI distracting. Its greatest value may be as a case study in how not to execute creature features on a limited budget.
