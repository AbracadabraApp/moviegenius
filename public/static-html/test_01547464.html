Last Men in Aleppo follows the White Helmets, volunteer rescue workers who risk their lives daily to save civilians in war-torn Syria. The film centers on Khaled and Mahmoud, two founding members who navigate the moral and physical dangers of their work while trying to maintain some semblance of normal life. Through their eyes, we witness the brutal reality of the Syrian Civil War, as they rush to bomb sites, dig through rubble, and save those they can while processing the loss of those they cannot.
Director Feras Fayyad's approach is remarkably intimate, embedding with the White Helmets to capture both the adrenaline of rescue operations and quiet moments of reflection. The film's power lies in its unflinching gaze and the trust developed between filmmaker and subjects, reminiscent of the embedded journalism in Restrepo (2010). The cinematography is immediate and visceral, placing viewers directly in the chaos and aftermath of airstrikes.
This documentary stands as a crucial historical document of the Syrian Civil War, joining other essential conflict documentaries like The Act of Killing (2012) and For Sama (2019). It challenges viewers to confront the human cost of war while highlighting the extraordinary courage of ordinary citizens. The film raises urgent questions about international intervention, civilian casualties, and the role of documentary filmmaking in crisis zones.
Within the war documentary genre, Last Men in Aleppo sets itself apart through its focus on civilian rescuers rather than combatants. It builds on the tradition of films like The Battle of Algiers (1966) and No Man's Land (1999) in examining the impact of conflict on civilian populations. The film's approach influenced later works like The Cave (2019) and Of Fathers and Sons (2017) in their intimate portrayal of life in conflict zones.
As conflicts continue worldwide, the film's examination of civilian courage and humanitarian response remains painfully relevant. It connects to contemporary discussions about intervention, refugee crises, and the role of citizen journalists and rescue workers in modern warfare, similar to themes explored in City of Ghosts (2017) and Fire at Sea (2016).
