Based on Peter Benchley's novel, 'The Island' follows journalist Blair Maynard (Michael Caine) investigating mysterious disappearances in the Caribbean. With his young son Justin in tow, he discovers a colony of pirates descended from 17th-century buccaneers, living in isolation and maintaining their brutal ancestral ways. What begins as an investigative story transforms into a survival thriller when the pirates kidnap Justin, attempting to indoctrinate him into their society while forcing Blair to watch his son's transformation.
Michael Caine delivers a committed performance, though even his considerable talents struggle with the script's inconsistencies. David Warner brings menace as the pirate leader Nau, but the film never fully capitalizes on its strong casting. Director Michael Ritchie, known for The Bad News Bears (1976), seems uncertain whether to emphasize horror, adventure, or social commentary, resulting in a tonally confused narrative.
The film attempts to explore themes of civilization versus savagery, similar to Lord of the Flies (1963), while commenting on the corruption of youth and the persistence of historical violence. However, these potentially rich themes are overshadowed by exploitation elements and uneven execution. The movie reflects early 1980s concerns about social breakdown and isolated communities, themes better explored in films like Escape from New York (1981).
Released in the wake of Benchley's more successful Jaws (1975) adaptation, 'The Island' attempts to replicate that film's mixture of adventure and terror but falls short. It sits uncomfortably between the serious maritime thrillers like The Deep (1977) and more exploitative fare of the era. The film's influence can be seen in later works like Cutthroat Island (1995), though mainly as a cautionary tale in pirate-themed filmmaking.
While modern piracy remains a real concern, as depicted in films like Captain Phillips (2013), 'The Island's' approach feels dated and sensationalistic. Its themes of isolated societies and cultural preservation might resonate with contemporary audiences, but these ideas are better explored in films like The Beach (2000) or Apocalypto (2006).
