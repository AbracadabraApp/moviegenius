Freaky brilliantly meshes the body-swap comedy premise with slasher horror conventions, following high school student Millie Kessler who switches bodies with the notorious Blissfield Butcher. The film cleverly subverts expectations by having a teenage girl trapped in a male serial killer's body while the killer uses her form to wreak havoc in her high school. The premise could have easily fallen flat, but the execution is both hilarious and genuinely unsettling.
Vince Vaughn delivers his best performance in years, masterfully portraying a teenage girl trapped in a killer's body with nuanced mannerisms and emotional depth. Kathryn Newton equally impresses, transforming from an insecure teenager to a menacing killer. Director Christopher Landon, following his success with Happy Death Day (2017), proves his talent for genre-bending horror-comedy, balancing genuine scares with laugh-out-loud moments.
The film tackles themes of gender roles, identity, and power dynamics in clever ways. It uses its premise to explore how society treats people differently based on their perceived gender and physical appearance, while never losing sight of its entertainment value. The high school setting allows for sharp commentary on teenage social dynamics and self-discovery.
Freaky stands on the shoulders of both body-swap classics like Freaky Friday (1976) and slasher staples like Friday the 13th (1980), creating something fresh and contemporary. It follows Blumhouse's successful formula of social commentary mixed with horror, similar to Get Out (2017), while maintaining the fun spirit of Scream (1996).
Released during the pandemic, the film offered much-needed entertainment while still delivering meaningful commentary on gender identity and power. It demonstrates how modern horror can be both socially conscious and thoroughly entertaining, following in the footsteps of films like Ready or Not (2019) and The Hunt (2020).
