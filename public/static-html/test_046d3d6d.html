This obscure documentary attempts to explore leadership principles through various case studies and interviews. However, the film suffers from a fragmented narrative structure and lacks compelling storytelling elements that would make its message more impactful.
The documentary's interview segments feel staged and lack authenticity. Unlike powerful documentaries such as The Fog of War (2003) or The Corporation (2003), this film fails to create meaningful connections between its subjects and viewers.
Released in 2004, the film emerged during a period of increased interest in leadership studies and corporate accountability following major scandals. However, it fails to capitalize on this zeitgeist in the way Enron: The Smartest Guys in the Room (2005) would successfully do a year later.
Within the business documentary genre, this film falls short of contemporaries like The Corporation (2003) and predecessors like Roger & Me (1989). It lacks both investigative depth and narrative engagement.
While leadership remains a crucial topic, modern viewers would be better served by more recent documentaries like Inside Job (2010) or The Big Short (2015) for insights into organizational leadership and its impact.
