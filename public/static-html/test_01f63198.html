Live and Let Live examines the motivations of six individuals who chose to adopt a vegan lifestyle, weaving their personal journeys with broader discussions about animal welfare, environmental sustainability, and ethical consumption. The documentary follows diverse subjects including a former butcher, a professional athlete, and an organic farmer, each offering unique perspectives on their transformation and the challenges they faced.
Director Marc Pierschel takes a refreshingly balanced approach, allowing his subjects to tell their stories without heavy-handed messaging. The film's strength lies in its intimate portrayal of personal change, reminiscent of the storytelling approach in Food, Inc. (2008) but with a more optimistic tone. The cinematography effectively captures both the personal moments and broader environmental contexts.
Unlike more confrontational documentaries like Earthlings (2005) or Cowspiracy (2014), Live and Let Live focuses on positive transformation and sustainable solutions. The film arrives at a crucial moment in food culture discourse, following the path paved by Super Size Me (2004) and Food Matters (2008) in examining our relationship with food, but with a distinctly modern perspective on plant-based living.
Within the food documentary genre, this film stands out for its human-centered approach, similar to Chef's Table (2015) in its focus on personal stories. It builds on the foundation laid by Forks Over Knives (2011) and Food Choices (2016), but offers a more intimate look at individual transformation rather than purely focusing on health statistics or environmental data.
The film's messages about sustainable living and ethical consumption have only grown more relevant since its release, particularly as climate change concerns intensify. Its themes parallel recent documentaries like The Game Changers (2018) and Seaspiracy (2021), contributing to an ongoing dialogue about food choices and environmental responsibility.
