When an alien spacecraft crashes in Moscow following a meteor shower, it triggers a complex chain of events centered around Yulia, a teenager whose life becomes intertwined with a mysterious extraterrestrial visitor. The story cleverly subverts typical invasion narratives by focusing on human reactions to the unknown, particularly through Yulia's evolving relationship with the alien visitor and her conflicted loyalty between her military father and her growing understanding of the visitors' peaceful intentions.
Irina Starshenbaum delivers a compelling performance as Yulia, bringing depth to her character's transformation from skepticism to understanding. Bondarchuk's direction shows remarkable restraint in balancing spectacular visual effects with intimate character moments, reminiscent of Denis Villeneuve's Arrival (2016). The film's technical achievements mark a significant leap forward for Russian science fiction cinema, rivaling Hollywood productions in scale and execution.
The film masterfully tackles themes of xenophobia and fear of the unknown, drawing parallels with contemporary social issues. Like District 9 (2009), it uses the alien presence as a mirror to examine human prejudices and social divisions. The Moscow setting provides a fresh perspective on the alien invasion genre, typically dominated by American narratives since War of the Worlds (1953).
Attraction represents a significant evolution in international science fiction, building on the foundation of films like Solaris (1972) while incorporating modern sensibilities seen in Edge of Tomorrow (2014). It shares thematic DNA with Close Encounters of the Third Kind (1977) in its exploration of first contact, but approaches the subject matter from a distinctly Russian cultural perspective.
The film's themes of overcoming prejudice and fear of the unknown resonate strongly in our current global climate. Its exploration of military response versus diplomatic engagement echoes contemporary international relations, while its emphasis on young people's role in bridging divides reflects modern social movements.
