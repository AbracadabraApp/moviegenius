Detective John Hobbes witnesses the execution of serial killer Edgar Reese, only to find similar murders continuing after Reese's death. The film masterfully unveils its supernatural premise - a demon named Azazel can transfer between human hosts through touch, leading to a cat-and-mouse game where anyone could be the enemy. Washington's portrayal of Hobbes grounds the supernatural elements in reality, making the threat feel immediate and personal.
Washington brings gravitas and humanity to Hobbes, making his growing paranoia and isolation deeply affecting. The supporting cast, particularly John Goodman as Hobbes' partner and Donald Sutherland as a veteran police lieutenant, adds layers of authenticity to the supernatural proceedings. Director Gregory Hoblit crafts a noir-tinged atmosphere reminiscent of Se7en (1995), while adding unique supernatural elements that set it apart from conventional crime thrillers.
Released in an era dominated by serial killer thrillers like The Silence of the Lambs (1991) and Kiss the Girls (1997), 'Fallen' distinguished itself by blending police procedural elements with supernatural horror. The film explores themes of faith, evil's perpetual nature, and the limitations of human justice against supernatural forces. Its examination of evil as an infectious force feels particularly relevant in today's interconnected world.
The film bridges the gap between supernatural horror like The Exorcist (1973) and detective noir like Angel Heart (1987). Its influence can be seen in later works like Constantine (2005) and Deliver Us from Evil (2014), which similarly combine detective work with demonic antagonists. The film's clever use of possession mechanics and unreliable narration set new standards for supernatural thrillers.
The film's themes of unseen evil moving through society remain startlingly relevant. Its exploration of trust and paranoia in law enforcement contexts resonates with contemporary discussions about institutional corruption and surveillance. The innovative narrative structure continues to influence modern thrillers, while its blend of genres feels fresh even by today's standards.
