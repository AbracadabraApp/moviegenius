Entre Nous follows two women in post-World War II France who form an intense friendship that transforms their lives. Lena (Isabelle Huppert) and Madeleine (Miou-Miou) meet in the 1950s, both married but unfulfilled. Their connection grows deeper as they share dreams, frustrations, and an entrepreneurial venture, eventually leading them to question their conventional lives. The film delicately balances their growing intimacy against the backdrop of their strained marriages and societal expectations.
Huppert and Miou-Miou deliver remarkably subtle performances that capture the complexity of female friendship and unspoken desire. Director Diane Kurys, drawing from her own family history, crafts scenes with documentary-like attention to period detail while maintaining emotional intimacy. The chemistry between the leads creates a palpable sense of connection that drives the narrative forward without melodrama.
The film provides a crucial perspective on post-war French society, particularly regarding women's roles and expectations. Like The Hours (2002) and Carol (2015), it explores female relationships within restrictive social frameworks. The story resonates with themes found in Jules and Jim (1962), examining how intimate relationships challenge societal norms, though here through a distinctly female lens.
Entre Nous stands as a significant entry in French feminist cinema, alongside works like Cléo from 5 to 7 (1962) and One Sings, the Other Doesn't (1977). It prefigures later explorations of female friendship such as Thelma & Louise (1991), though with a distinctly European sensibility that favors nuance over dramatic confrontation.
The film's exploration of female agency and friendship remains startlingly relevant, particularly in its depiction of women seeking fulfillment beyond traditional family structures. Its themes of personal liberation and emotional authenticity echo in contemporary works like Portrait of a Lady on Fire (2019) and Nomadland (2020).
