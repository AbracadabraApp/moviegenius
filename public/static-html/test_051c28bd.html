In a remote rural Chinese village during the Cultural Revolution, a young educated youth named Lao Gar is sent to teach at a poverty-stricken school. Finding himself without textbooks or traditional teaching materials, he begins improvising lessons by teaching his students to write the Chinese characters they know, encouraging them to create their own texts based on their experiences. This unorthodox approach challenges both educational norms and political expectations, leading to tension with local authorities.
Xie Yuan delivers a nuanced performance as Lao Gar, capturing the character's idealism and growing disillusionment. Chen Kaige's direction, part of the influential Fifth Generation of Chinese filmmakers, creates a visual poetry that elevates the simple narrative into a profound meditation on knowledge and power. The cinematography by Zhang Yimou, who would later direct Raise the Red Lantern (1991), transforms the rural setting into a canvas of striking imagery and metaphorical depth.
The film masterfully explores themes of cultural identity and education during a period of immense social upheaval. Like Farewell My Concubine (1993) and To Live (1994), it examines the impact of political movements on individual lives. The struggle between traditional knowledge and revolutionary ideology mirrors similar tensions in works like The Blue Kite (1993), while its focus on rural education recalls Not One Less (1999).
As part of the Chinese Fifth Generation cinema movement, 'King of the Children' stands alongside other seminal works like Yellow Earth (1984) and Red Sorghum (1987) in its innovative visual style and subtle political critique. The film's approach to depicting rural life and education influenced later works such as The Story of Qiu Ju (1992) and Primary Love (2000).
The film's examination of educational reform and cultural preservation remains startlingly relevant today. Its questions about the relationship between language, identity, and power echo contemporary debates about educational standardization and cultural authenticity. The tension between traditional knowledge and modern progress resonates with current discussions about educational methodology and cultural preservation.
