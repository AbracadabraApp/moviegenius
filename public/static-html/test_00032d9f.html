Clay returns from college to find his ex-girlfriend Blair and best friend Julian caught in a downward spiral of drugs and decadence in Los Angeles. The film strips away the glossy veneer of privileged Beverly Hills youth to reveal a darker reality of addiction, betrayal, and lost innocence. Unlike the more graphic novel by Bret Easton Ellis, the film version focuses on Clay's desperate attempts to save Julian from self-destruction while navigating his complicated feelings for Blair.
Robert Downey Jr. delivers a devastating performance as Julian, eerily foreshadowing his own real-life struggles with addiction. His portrayal of a privileged kid's descent into drug dependency is raw and unflinching. Andrew McCarthy effectively embodies Clay's mounting desperation, while Jami Gertz brings depth to Blair's conflicted loyalties. Director Marek Kanievska and cinematographer Edward Lachman create a neon-noir atmosphere that turns Los Angeles into a beautiful but dangerous playground.
The film captures the dark side of Reagan-era excess, showing how wealth and privilege can't protect against addiction and moral decay. It stands as a crucial counterpoint to the decade's materialism, alongside Wall Street (1987) and Bright Lights, Big City (1988). The movie's exploration of youth alienation and substance abuse parallels other contemporary cautionary tales like River's Edge (1986).
While often categorized with Brat Pack films, Less Than Zero subverts the genre's typically lighter approach seen in St. Elmo's Fire (1985) and Pretty in Pink (1986). It shares more DNA with darker youth narratives like Christiane F. (1981) and would influence later addiction dramas like Requiem for a Dream (2000).
The film's themes of privilege, addiction, and failed intervention remain painfully relevant amid today's opioid crisis. Its critique of wealth's inability to buy happiness echoes through contemporary works like Euphoria (2019) and Beautiful Boy (2018). Downey Jr.'s performance gains additional poignancy given his subsequent career resurrection.
