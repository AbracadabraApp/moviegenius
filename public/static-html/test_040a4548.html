A young woman (Jessie Buckley) travels with her boyfriend Jake (Jesse Plemons) to meet his parents during a snowstorm, all while contemplating ending their relationship. What begins as a seemingly straightforward narrative morphs into a surreal exploration of identity, time, and memory. The story deliberately destabilizes viewer expectations, using shifting characters and temporal inconsistencies to create a dreamlike atmosphere that mirrors the psychological deterioration at the film's core.
Kaufman's direction creates a masterclass in psychological tension, reminiscent of Mulholland Drive (2001) in its reality-bending approach. Jessie Buckley delivers a tour-de-force performance as the unnamed protagonist, her character subtly transforming throughout the film. Toni Collette and David Thewlis as Jake's parents provide unsettling performances that age and de-age inexplicably, adding to the film's destabilizing atmosphere.
The film explores profound themes of loneliness, identity, and the nature of memory in the modern age. Like Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind (2004), it uses surreal elements to examine deep emotional truths. The integration of references to art, literature, and film creates a rich tapestry of cultural commentary, particularly regarding masculinity and intellectual pretension.
Within the psychological thriller genre, it pushes boundaries like Under the Skin (2013) and Mother! (2017), creating a new subcategory of existential horror. The film's structure deliberately subverts traditional narrative expectations, following in the tradition of Synecdoche, New York (2008) while creating something entirely unique.
In an era of increasing isolation and digital disconnection, the film's exploration of loneliness and failed human connection resonates deeply. Its examination of memory and identity in the digital age parallels themes found in Black Mirror episodes, while its surreal approach to mental health issues reflects growing social awareness of psychological struggles.
