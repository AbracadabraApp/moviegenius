Basic follows Tom Hardy (Travolta), a DEA agent investigating the mysterious disappearance of Army Rangers during a training exercise in Panama. When two survivors return with conflicting stories about what happened to their sergeant (Jackson), Hardy must untangle a web of deception alongside Captain Julia Osborne (Nielsen). The film attempts to channel the complexity of Rashomon (1950) with multiple perspectives and unreliable narrators, but quickly becomes bogged down in its own labyrinthine plot twists.
Despite reuniting Pulp Fiction (1994) co-stars Travolta and Jackson, their chemistry is largely wasted as they share minimal screen time. McTiernan, known for action classics like Die Hard (1988), struggles to maintain narrative coherence while juggling multiple storylines and revelations. The talented cast does their best with the material, but the constantly shifting perspectives and double-crosses ultimately undermine their performances.
Released during the early years of the War on Terror, Basic attempts to explore themes of military corruption and the fog of war, but its Byzantine plot mechanics overshadow any meaningful commentary. Unlike contemporary military thrillers like The Hurt Locker (2008) or Black Hawk Down (2001), the film fails to ground its story in authentic military culture or genuine human drama.
The military investigation thriller genre had seen stronger entries like A Few Good Men (1992) and Courage Under Fire (1996), which better balanced complex narratives with character development. Basic seems more influenced by the twist-heavy plotting of films like The Usual Suspects (1995), but lacks the careful groundwork that made those revelations satisfying.
The film's approach to military storytelling feels dated compared to more nuanced modern examinations like Zero Dark Thirty (2012). Its reliance on plot twists over character development and thematic depth makes it feel more like a product of the late 90s thriller boom than a meaningful addition to the military drama genre.
