10 follows successful composer George Webber (Dudley Moore) through a midlife crisis triggered by his obsession with Jenny (Bo Derek), a beautiful young woman he spots on her way to her wedding. The film's genius lies in how it transforms a potentially creepy premise into a thoughtful exploration of aging, desire, and self-delusion. George's pursuit of Jenny to Mexico, leaving behind his sensible girlfriend Sam (Julie Andrews), becomes both a comedy of errors and a journey of self-discovery.
Blake Edwards directs with the same sophisticated touch he brought to Breakfast at Tiffany's (1961), balancing comedy with genuine emotional depth. Dudley Moore delivers a breakthrough performance that defined his Hollywood career, making George simultaneously pathetic and endearing. Bo Derek became an overnight sensation, with her beach running scene becoming one of cinema's most iconic moments. Julie Andrews brings crucial gravitas as the voice of reason.
The film arrived at a pivotal moment in American culture, as the sexual revolution of the 1970s gave way to the materialism of the 1980s. It sparked discussions about beauty standards, aging, and male midlife crisis that influenced later films like American Beauty (1999) and Lost in Translation (2003). The term '10' entered popular culture as a rating for physical perfection, while simultaneously critiquing such superficial standards.
10 revolutionized the romantic comedy genre by introducing more mature themes and complex character motivations. It paved the way for sophisticated adult comedies like When Harry Met Sally (1989) and Manhattan (1979), proving that romantic comedies could tackle serious subjects while remaining entertaining. The film's influence can be seen in later works like About Schmidt (2002) and Crazy, Stupid, Love (2011).
The film's examination of beauty standards, age gaps in relationships, and midlife crisis remains startlingly relevant. Modern audiences might see parallels with discussions about social media's impact on beauty standards and the commodification of youth. Its sophisticated approach to comedy and relationships still feels fresh compared to many contemporary romantic comedies.
