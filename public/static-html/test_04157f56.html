Honey Boy delivers a searing autobiographical portrait of Shia LaBeouf's troubled relationship with his father, with LaBeouf himself playing the role of his own dad. The narrative weaves between two timelines: a young child actor Otis (Noah Jupe) living with his volatile father in a motel, and an older Otis (Lucas Hedges) in rehab confronting his past trauma. The meta-theatrical element of LaBeouf playing his own father adds layers of complexity to this deeply personal story of reconciliation and healing.
LaBeouf's transformation into his father is nothing short of remarkable, channeling both the charm and menace of a failed rodeo clown struggling with addiction. Noah Jupe delivers a heartbreaking performance as young Otis, capturing the confusion and resilience of a child forced to parent his own parent. Director Alma Har'el brings a dreamlike quality to the proceedings, blending memory and reality in ways reminiscent of The Florida Project (2017) and Moonlight (2016).
The film offers a brutal examination of child stardom that stands alongside I, Tonya (2017) and The Tale (2018) in its unflinching portrayal of trauma. It speaks to broader themes of toxic masculinity, addiction, and the cyclical nature of abuse, while also serving as a form of public therapy for LaBeouf himself. The film's exploration of father-son dynamics recalls the raw emotion of Manchester by the Sea (2016).
Within the autobiography genre, Honey Boy pushes boundaries by blending elements of therapy, performance art, and traditional narrative. It shares DNA with confessional works like Pain and Glory (2019) and 8½ (1963), but brings a uniquely millennial perspective to the form. The film's approach to childhood trauma through an artistic lens recalls The Tree of Life (2011).
In an era of increasing openness about mental health and childhood trauma, Honey Boy serves as a powerful testament to the healing potential of art. Its examination of toxic father-son relationships and the price of early fame remains deeply relevant, especially as conversations about child actors' welfare continue to evolve. The film's therapeutic approach to storytelling has influenced works like Malcolm & Marie (2021).
