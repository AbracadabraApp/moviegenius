Comet follows Dell and Kimberly across six years of their relationship in multiple parallel universes. The story begins at a meteor shower viewing in LA's Hollywood Forever Cemetery, where their chance meeting sets off a complex exploration of love, timing, and destiny. The narrative jumps between different moments and possible realities of their relationship, challenging viewers to piece together the true nature of their connection.
Emmy Rossum and Justin Long deliver career-defining performances as the cynical Dell and the free-spirited Kimberly. Their chemistry crackles with authentic tension and vulnerability, making their philosophical debates and emotional confrontations feel genuinely lived-in. First-time director Sam Esmail (who later created Mr. Robot) brings a distinct visual style that mirrors the fractured narrative, using shifting aspect ratios and color palettes to distinguish between timeline shifts.
The film arrives at a fascinating intersection between indie romance and science fiction, following in the footsteps of Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind (2004) and 500 Days of Summer (2009). It speaks to millennial anxieties about commitment, destiny, and the paradox of choice in modern relationships. The parallel universe concept serves as a perfect metaphor for the what-ifs that haunt modern romance.
Comet reinvents the romantic drama by incorporating elements of quantum physics and parallel realities, similar to how Inception (2010) revolutionized the heist film. It shares DNA with Mr. Nobody (2009) and Sliding Doors (1998) in its exploration of multiple possible lives, while maintaining the intimate focus of films like Before Sunset (2004).
In an era of dating apps and infinite choice, Comet's meditation on fate versus free will feels more relevant than ever. Its examination of how technology and modern life impact our ability to form meaningful connections echoes themes later explored in Her (2013) and Black Mirror episodes. The film's innovative structure has influenced recent works like The Last Five Years (2014) and La La Land (2016).
