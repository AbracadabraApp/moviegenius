Undone follows Alma, a young woman who discovers she can manipulate time following a near-fatal car accident. When her deceased father appears to her, claiming his death wasn't accidental, Alma begins an extraordinary journey questioning reality, time, and her own sanity. The show's innovative use of rotoscope animation, similar to A Scanner Darkly (2006), creates a dreamlike atmosphere that perfectly matches its reality-bending narrative.
Rosa Salazar delivers a tour-de-force performance that transcends the animation, bringing remarkable nuance to Alma's complex emotional journey. Bob Odenkirk as her father provides gravitas and mystery, while the rotoscope animation technique, reminiscent of Waking Life (2001), creates a visual language that perfectly captures the show's exploration of consciousness and reality. The seamless blend of live-action performance and animation echoes the groundbreaking approach of Tower (2016).
The series masterfully tackles mental health, indigenous spirituality, and family dynamics with sensitivity and depth. Like Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind (2004), it uses surreal elements to explore very real emotional truths. The show's representation of mental health challenges recalls Silver Linings Playbook (2012), while its exploration of family trauma shares DNA with The Butterfly Effect (2004).
Undone pushes the boundaries of adult animation, joining works like Persepolis (2007) in using the medium to tell sophisticated, mature stories. Its blend of science fiction and personal drama recalls Arrival (2016), while its treatment of time and reality manipulation shares themes with Donnie Darko (2001).
The show's examination of mental health, generational trauma, and cultural identity resonates strongly with contemporary discussions. Its innovative animation technique has influenced subsequent productions, while its nuanced portrayal of family relationships and personal identity continues to feel urgent and relevant.
