Baaghi follows Saajan Sood (Salman Khan), a street-smart young man who falls in love with Kaajal (Nagma), a naive girl forced into the world of dance bars. The film's narrative weaves together elements of romance, action, and social commentary as Saajan fights against a powerful criminal network to protect Kaajal. The story, while following familiar Bollywood tropes, stands out for its energetic execution and emotional depth, particularly in how it handles the rescue-romance narrative without completely diminishing its female lead's agency.
This film marks a crucial point in Salman Khan's career, cementing his image as the action hero with a heart of gold - a persona that would define many of his later successes. Nagma delivers a compelling debut performance, bringing vulnerability and strength to her character. Director Deepak Shivdasani crafts a narrative that balances commercial elements with emotional storytelling, while the musical numbers by Anand-Milind became chartbusters of their time.
Baaghi tackles several social issues prevalent in 1990s India, particularly the exploitation of young women in metropolitan cities. While its treatment might seem dated now, it was among the mainstream films of its time to address human trafficking and the dance bar culture of Mumbai. The film's portrayal of these issues, though dramatized, helped bring these conversations into public discourse.
Released during the transition period of Indian cinema from the 1980s to the 1990s, Baaghi represents the evolution of the masala film genre. It follows the tradition of films like Tezaab (1988) and Ghayal (1990), where action sequences are balanced with strong emotional cores. The film's influence can be seen in later works like Khal Nayak (1993) and Rangeela (1995), which similarly combined social issues with commercial elements.
While the film's treatment of certain issues might feel dated, its core themes of fighting against exploitation and standing up for justice remain relevant. The film's exploration of urban crime and corruption continues to resonate, though modern films like Pink (2016) and Gangubai Kathiawadi (2022) approach similar themes with more nuance and complexity.
